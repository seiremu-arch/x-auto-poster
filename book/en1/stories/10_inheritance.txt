X. Inheritance


I

　It was on the third page of the schedule of assets.

　　　Held by the Dream Editing Bureau: memory fragment data, one set
　　　Deceased: Tsukahara Shigeo
　　　Storage commenced: 22 years ago
　　　Assessed value: ¥0

　The value field is zero because sale is prohibited, the judicial scrivener explained. It cannot be transferred or pledged. It can be inherited. It can be inherited, but no price attaches to it.

　"The heirs will need to make a choice," the scrivener said. "Whether to continue storage, or to have it destroyed."

　"Does continuing cost anything?"

　"There is an annual management fee." He turned the page. "Twelve thousand yen. Payable by the family."

　My grandfather died at eighty-nine. He had been using the Dream Editing Bureau for twenty-two years. That is not unusual in the old. Once people reach the age when sleep grows thin, a good many go to have the weight of their nightmares brought down.

　There were three heirs. My father was already dead, and my aunt showed no interest. The one who took on the paperwork was me, the grandson.

　I did not know my grandfather well. As a child I saw him at New Year and at the midsummer festival. He taught me shogi and I never got good at it. After my father died, it came down to meeting once a year at my grandmother's house.

　At the wake, my aunt said:

　"Father never said anything, right to the end, did he."

　It was not spoken against him. It had the sound of confirming a fact. Nobody contradicted it, so I suppose it was a fact.


II

　Viewing takes place on the third floor of the Bureau.

　I was shown into a private room and seated at a terminal. The member of staff explained the procedure and then left.

　"Beginning the viewing," the Proofreader said. A voice with no inflection. From the first few words I could not determine whether I was dealing with a person.

　Bands of colour lined up on the monitor.

　"What an heir may view is the composition of the bands and the information attached to the fragments in the record," the Proofreader said. "The contents cannot be replayed. No technique exists for extracting a dream as an image."

　"Then what can be seen?"

　"When the memory is from. Where it is of. Who appears in it. Those three."

　Those appearing were shown as identifiers. The per-person numbers the Bureau administers; no names come out. My grandmother's identifier was obvious at once. The highest frequency. Next my father, then my aunt.

　My own identifier was there as well, further down. I had appeared in my grandfather's dreams forty-seven times.

　"Is that on the high side?"

　"The mean for a grandchild is thirty-eight," the Proofreader said. "That is the mean where they do not live in the same household."

　Nine above the mean. What that meant I did not know. But there was something in the mere arrival of a figure that made me brace a little. Until today I had been able to go without knowing how many times I had appeared inside my grandfather's sleep. A thing one had been able to go without knowing comes out in ten minutes of sitting in a chair.

　It was that kind of asset.


III

　At first I thought I had miscounted.

　I sorted the identifier list by frequency and ran my finger down from the top. First, my grandmother. Second, my father. Third, my aunt. So far, as expected.

　At the fourth, my finger stopped.

　A number I did not know. The frequency was over two thousand. Nearly three times my aunt's.

　"This identifier —"

　"That cannot be disclosed," the Proofreader said. "A third party's identifier cannot be enquired into without the consent of the person or of their heirs."

　"Can you give me the period in which they appeared?"

　"The period is information in the record. It can be disclosed."

　A line appeared. From forty-nine years ago to forty-four years ago, concentrated in five years. After that it broke off abruptly.

　My father was born forty-seven years ago.

　There was one more number beside that identifier. The frequency was low. It appeared from forty-six years ago to forty-four years ago. About two and a half years.

　"Is there any relation between these two identifiers?"

　"Records of relation are not included in the fragments," the Proofreader said. "The frequency with which they appear simultaneously in the same scene can be calculated, however."

　"Calculate it."

　"Ninety-four per cent."

　Which meant the two were always together.

　I leant back in the chair. The lower-frequency identifier, appearing forty-six years ago and gone in two and a half. There is no record of age. But the reasons an adult disappears from someone's dreams in two and a half years, and the reasons a child does, are not usually the same.


IV

　"Tell me the enquiry procedure."

　"An identifier enquiry from an heir has two stages," the Proofreader said. "At the first stage we notify the other party of nothing but the existence of an intention to enquire. Where the other party responds, at the second stage both parties' contact details are disclosed."

　"The other party being?"

　"The person behind that identifier, or, where the person is deceased, their heirs."

　"And if they do not respond?"

　"Nothing happens," the Proofreader said. "The fact that an enquiry was made does not remain in the record either."

　I was quiet for a moment.

　"One question," I said. "Has this enquiry ever been made before?"

　"It has," the Proofreader said. "One instance, three years ago."

　"By whom?"

　"That cannot be disclosed."

　"The direction," I said. "Was it sent from this side, or did it come from theirs?"

　"From theirs," the Proofreader said. "Three years ago, an enquiry concerning Tsukahara Shigeo was made by an heir on that identifier's side."

　"Did my grandfather respond?"

　"He did not."

　"You mean he refused?"

　"Non-response," the Proofreader said. "Where no reply is given by the deadline, it is processed as non-response. That is a separate category from refusal."

　"What is the point of making it a separate category?"

　"Refusal is an expression of intention," the Proofreader said. "Non-response makes no determination as to whether there was an expression of intention."

　"From their side, surely it is the same."

　"What is notified to their side is only the fact that no response was obtained," the Proofreader said. "The category is not notified."

　Three years ago my grandfather was eighty-six. There is no record of cognitive decline. He could read letters and he went out.

　So my grandfather, in all probability, read it. Read it, and did nothing.

　Doing nothing is a category of its own in this scheme. And that category is not conveyed to the other side. What reached them must have been one line, saying that no response was obtained.


V

　My grandmother is eighty-five and still in her own house.

　When I called in at the weekend I sat in front of the household altar. The photograph of my grandfather was from his seventies.

　"They tell me you went to the Bureau," my grandmother said, setting out tea.

　"Paperwork."

　"A nuisance, I expect."

　"A nuisance."

　She made tea for herself and sat down opposite me.

　"Your grandfather used to say he'd stopped dreaming," she said. "He'd had too much shaved off, he said, and laughed about it."

　"Did he."

　"He used to have bad nights, long ago. When he was young." She said it easily. "Up to his forties. I do wonder what that was."

　I drank my tea. It was hot.

　"You didn't ask him?"

　"Of course I asked," my grandmother said. "Many times. And every time, he didn't remember."

　"Do you think he really didn't remember?"

　"Who can say." She cupped the teacup in both hands. "That man had a particular face for saying he didn't remember. He'd turn his head slightly to one side. So after a while I decided not to ask."

　She smiled a little. It was not a smile with blame in it.

　"Fifty years together, you know, and a few things turn up that are easier not to ask about."

　Beside the altar there is a stack of old albums. She has shown them to me several times. My grandfather and grandmother, my father and my aunt. Photographs of the four of them. I have never seen one with anybody else in it.

　"Grandma," I said.

　"Yes?"

　The words would not go on.

　"No. Nothing."

　She smiled a little and drank her tea. Whether I had any right to bring into this house a thing this person does not know, I did not know. Nor whether not knowing is this person's happiness. And it did not seem to me that a grandson was qualified to decide it.

　As I was leaving she came out to the door.

　"You'll be keeping your grandfather's, then."

　"Probably."

　"It costs money, doesn't it."

　"Twelve thousand yen. A year."

　She thought for a moment, and nodded.

　"That's not so dear."


VI

　The procedure was a single screen.

　Three options side by side. Continue storage, destroy, identifier enquiry. You cannot go on to the next screen without choosing one.

　Choose to continue storage and I go on paying twelve thousand yen a year. My grandfather's dreams stay put somewhere in the Bureau, with nobody seeing the contents.

　Choose destruction and it ends there. The two thousand entries from forty-nine to forty-four years ago, and the lower-frequency identifier that only appears for two and a half years, go together.

　Choose the enquiry and a notification goes out. The same notification that came this way once, three years ago, going the other way.

　I looked at the screen.

　"One question," I said. "What happens if I choose nothing?"

　"If the storage fee goes unpaid for three years, destruction is automatic," the Proofreader said. "No confirmation of the heir's intention is carried out."

　"So not deciding isn't possible."

　"No option of not deciding is provided," the Proofreader said. "Not deciding produces the same result as destruction."

　Leave it unchosen and it goes. That seemed unlike any of the three options, and came out as the second one. The same, and yet on the form it does not count as having chosen.

　Three years ago my grandfather was probably in that position. He read it and did nothing. He was put in the category of non-response. The Proofreader says that is a separate category from refusal, but from the other side it would have looked the same.

　I sat looking at the screen for a while.

　Two thousand fragments, ranged between forty-nine and forty-four years ago. Nobody can see the contents. Whether they were happy or painful is not knowable. Only the fact remains, as a figure, that in those five years the one who appeared most often inside my grandfather's sleep was not my grandmother.

　And the lower-frequency identifier disappears after two and a half years.

　After it disappeared, my grandfather had bad nights into his forties. My grandmother remembers it, and says, I do wonder what that was. Until he died at eighty-nine, my grandfather never told anybody the reason. He settled it with a laugh and too much shaved off.

　Shaving is possible. Erasing is not.

　I chose to continue storage.

　Why I did not choose the enquiry, and why I did not choose destruction, I cannot explain well. Only, it seemed to me that it did not have to be me who decided. While I hold it, it does not disappear. That much is what I have bought for twelve thousand yen a year.

　"Continuation of storage accepted," the Proofreader said. "The next invoice will be the same month next year."

　"There is something I would like to write down," I said. "Is there a free-text field?"

　"There is," the Proofreader said. "Free text entered by an heir does not go into the statistics. It is not displayed to the next heir either."

　"Then who reads it?"

　"I read it," the Proofreader said. "They are records that may be discarded. There is no record of why they have not been."

　I wrote three lines there.

　— I know that an enquiry was made three years ago. My grandmother does not. This year I chose storage.

　I finished writing and closed the terminal.

　From the third-floor window you could see the Bureau's car park. A few cars were parked, and from one of them a young man was getting out. He might be somebody's heir and he might not. People of that sort go in and out of this building, several a day.

　Next year I shall pay twelve thousand yen again. In the meantime my grandfather's five years do not disappear. Nobody sees them, and they are there.

　Whether a thing that is there unseen may be called remaining, I have not yet decided.
