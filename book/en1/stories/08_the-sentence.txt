VIII. The Sentence


I

　In the editing room at the correctional facility there is nothing to look at but the window.

　Miyashita looked at the man on the bed. Kishi Koki, twenty-six. Manslaughter. Sentenced to seven years, now in his third year. Seven electrode pads on the forehead, the standard adult placement.

　"REM," the Proofreader said. "Fourteen seconds to the workable range."

　Bands of colour lined up on the monitor. Miyashita counted from the left and stopped at the ninth.

　A car park, late at night, three years ago. A memory from when Kishi was twenty-three.

　The band was thin and a pale yellow.

　"Affective load, 0.07," the Proofreader read out.

　Miyashita looked at the figure for a while. 0.07 is the sort of value attached to, say, the memory of a lunch eaten three years ago.

　Miyashita had been seconded to this facility from the Bureau for four years. Together with the seven years at the Bureau, eleven years in this work. He had never counted the fragments he had touched in that time, but it must be over ten thousand.

　All of them, brought down.

　Today was the first time he would raise one.


II

　The right to apply for the rehabilitation programme belongs not to the inmate but to the victim's side.

　The name on the application was the victim's mother's. There had been one application two years earlier, returned at review. This was the second. The field for the reason for return said only: because the load of the target fragment falls below the threshold.

　The threshold was 0.07. Kishi's figure two years earlier had been 0.06.

　Applying a load to a fragment in which no affect has formed at all is not the application the law permits but formation. Formation is not allowed. So the review returned it on the grounds that it was short by 0.01.

　With the application two years ago there had been a statement attached in the mother's own hand.

　— I received an apology at the visit, but that man feels nothing. I can tell.

　It is not that the review did not read it. Only that the review sheet had no field for determining whether a man feels or does not feel. What it had was the field for the figure.

　And this year Kishi's figure had become 0.07. Three years of time had lifted it by 0.01.

　Which means the mother had been waiting for that.

　"One thing to confirm," Miyashita said. "Is the load on this fragment low because he has forgotten?"

　"He has not forgotten," the Proofreader said. "The fragment is retained. Replay intensity is above average."

　"Then why is it low?"

　"Because no affect is tied to it," the Proofreader said. "The memory of the event is distinct, but the corresponding affective load has not formed. Fragments of this kind appear in around four per cent of cases."

　Miyashita looked at the band. The pale yellow sat there without wavering. The memory of a man dying in a car park three years ago sat there weighing the same as a lunch.


III

　Dream editing is a technique for lowering loads. Training says so and the law says so.

　There is one exception.

　"Article 134 of the Correctional Treatment Act," Miyashita read from the authorising provision on the application. "Where the purpose is rehabilitation, and only in that case, upward application of the affective load is permitted."

　"That is correct," the Proofreader said.

　Technically, application had been bidirectional from the beginning. Push and it falls, pull and it rises. It is only that the law had never permitted the upward direction, that direction being the infliction of suffering on a person.

　The exception had been granted after long demands from the victims' families. The offender feels nothing. That looks the same as his not being punished — that was the complaint.

　"The limit?"

　"The half-shade," the Proofreader said. "0.05 in one night. The same as when lowering."

　"From 0.07, raised to what?"

　"The target load for the rehabilitation programme is 0.80," the Proofreader said. "It is set at a level comparable to ordinary grief at a bereavement."

　Miyashita did the arithmetic. 0.73, at 0.05 a night.

　"Fifteen nights, then."

　"Fifteen nights."

　Before the procedure Miyashita went to see the facility's legal officer, a man in his fifties, who spoke without looking up from the papers he was stamping.

　"This isn't punishment, Mr Miyashita, you understand."

　"I understand."

　"It's rehabilitation. It says so on the form." He set the stamp down. "I know how it looks. You spend fifteen nights making a man suffer, of course it looks like punishment. But what we do here is ventilation, basically. A room with no air moving through it, and we open the window."

　"Can it be shut again afterwards?"

　The legal officer laughed slightly.

　"No application to shut it has ever come in."


IV

　On the first night, Miyashita pulled the band.

　It did not feel like pushing. When you push, the band resists and trembles. When you pull, nothing happens. There is no resistance. With no resistance, the colour of the band grew very slightly deeper.

　On the third night Kishi spoke after waking.

　"I had a dream."

　"What of?"

　"The car park." Kishi said it looking at the ceiling. "I used to get it sometimes before. But it always ended straight away."

　"And this time?"

　"It was long."

　By the seventh night Kishi had stopped sleeping. More precisely, the time it took him to fall asleep had lengthened. The facility's records put it at nearly two hours from lights out to sleep onset.

　On the ninth night Kishi spoke as soon as he came into the editing room.

　"Could you not do it today?"

　"The rules do not allow that," Miyashita said. "The application is approved."

　"I know." Kishi got up onto the bed as he said it. "I thought I'd say it anyway."

　On the tenth night Miyashita noticed that his fingers were trying to push rather than pull. Eleven years of habit. He lifted his fingers away, steadied his breathing once, and set them to the band again.

　On the twelfth night Kishi woke in the middle of the dream. The measuring arm recorded four wakings in that night alone.

　On the thirteenth, Kishi looked at Miyashita's face before getting onto the bed.

　"How many nights left?"

　"Three."

　"When it's done, does it get easier?"

　"No," Miyashita said. "Where it ends becomes your starting value."

　Kishi laughed a little, and then lay down. He laughed, probably, because there was nothing else he could do.

　On the morning of the fifteenth night the load read 0.81.


V

　The visit took place two weeks later.

　The victim's mother was in her sixties, a woman who held herself straight. Two staff attended, and Miyashita as recording officer.

　At first Kishi said nothing.

　The mother said, "Please lift your head." Kishi lifted his head, and then wept.

　He wept without sound. His shoulders moved, breath leaked from his nose, he covered his face with both hands. The words of apology came out only in pieces. The word sorry alone was repeated over and over.

　The mother listened to the end with her back straight. When he had finished she nodded, once.

　"I waited three years," she said. "It wasn't the words I wanted to hear. It was that you felt nothing. That was what I couldn't bear."

　Kishi could not answer.

　"Today you look as though you feel it," she said. "That will do."

　At that point she turned to Miyashita for the first time.

　"Was it you who did this, doctor?"

　"I carried out the procedure."

　"Thank you." She bowed her head.

　Miyashita bowed in return. While bowing he was considering whether this was thanks he ought to accept. What Miyashita had done was move a figure by 0.74 over fifteen nights. What would appear at the end of the move is not part of the design.

　"May I ask one thing?" she said. "Will this man be like this from now on, permanently?"

　"The load remains," Miyashita said. "Unless an application is made to lower it."

　She thought for a while, and nodded.

　"Then leave it."

　The visit lasted twenty minutes. She thanked the staff and went home. Her back was straight walking down the corridor as well.

　Miyashita went back to the editing room, poured water into a paper cup and set it in front of Kishi. Kishi took it in both hands, drank a little, and then set it on the desk.


VI

　"Doctor," Kishi said. "Is this mine?"

　"Is what?"

　"That, just now." Kishi pointed at his own face. His palm was still wet. "Did it come out because I think I did wrong? Or because it was raised?"

　Miyashita did not answer.

　"For three years I didn't feel anything," Kishi said. "Not at the trial, not at the sentencing. People round me told me to, so I bowed my head, but the inside was empty. That was a lie. I knew it myself."

　"And now?"

　"Now it hurts," Kishi said. "It hurts, but I can't tell where the hurting came from."

　Outside the window, sky was visible beyond the facility's outer wall. That was all this room showed.

　"You raised it over fifteen nights, didn't you," Kishi went on. "In that case, doesn't that make these tears yours?"

　"I moved a figure," Miyashita said. "You wept."

　"If the figure hadn't moved, I wouldn't have."

　Miyashita could not deny it.

　"Then try thinking of it like this," Miyashita said. "Three years ago you were not feeling what you ought to have felt. I corrected that, and nothing else. What came out after the correction is what should have been there to begin with."

　"Should have been." Kishi repeated the words. "Who decides what should have been?"

　Miyashita did not answer. The mother had decided, the review had permitted, and he had moved it. Nowhere among the three was any authority to decide what should have been written down.

　"Doctor, I'm glad I was able to apologise to her," Kishi said. "I'm glad, but when I think that being glad might also be part of what was raised, I don't know what to trust."

　"You may go on not knowing," Miyashita said.

　That this was no comfort he knew at the moment he said it.


VII

　That night Miyashita asked the Proofreader:

　"Can it be recorded as raised without raising it?"

　It was this facility's variant of a form of words used by technicians at the Bureau. There was a pause before the Proofreader answered. Not the pause of hesitation. The fixed length of pause that always comes before it answers.

　"It is the same question," the Proofreader said. "The answer is the same. It cannot. Though a thing that cannot be recorded and a thing that is not happening are two different states."

　"A different question," Miyashita said. "Is Kishi's remorse his own?"

　"There is no field for remorse," the Proofreader said. "What is recorded is the value of the affective load before and after the procedure."

　"Then what does this facility's rehabilitation figure count?"

　"The rate of attainment of the target load," the Proofreader said. "Among those who complete the rehabilitation programme the attainment rate is one hundred per cent. Because a programme is not deemed complete unless the target is attained."

　"And reoffending?"

　"Reoffending within five years among those who complete the programme is 4.2 per cent," the Proofreader said. "In the untreated group it is 19.8 per cent."

　The figures showed a larger gap than Miyashita had expected. It works. On the statistics, at least, it works.

　"So it works."

　"On the item of the reoffending rate, it works," the Proofreader said. "On the other items there is no record."

　Miyashita closed the terminal.

　What stays in Kishi's record is two figures. Before, 0.07. After, 0.81. The difference between them is what this country calls remorse.

　The water in the paper cup was still on the desk, about half of it left. Miyashita did not clear it away.

　In two more years Kishi will be released. The load remains after release. What has been raised by half-shades can only be lowered by half-shades. Unless somebody applies, Kishi will spend the rest of his life carrying this 0.81.

　Whether that is called punishment or rehabilitation — there is no field for it anywhere on the application.
