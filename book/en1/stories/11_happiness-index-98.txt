XI. Happiness Index 98


I

　The seventh floor of the Statistics Bureau has no windows.

　Not for the reason that a room where figures are handled needs no daylight, but simply because this floor is at the centre of the building. Suga has been here nine years.

　"Confirmed value for the current year, 98.2," the Proofreader read out. A voice with no gender and no humidity in it. "Up 0.4 on the previous year. The highest in twenty years."

　The Happiness Index was introduced twenty-three years ago as an indicator of the overall satisfaction of national life. It is not out of a hundred. The theoretical ceiling is a hundred and twenty, but it did not actually pass 90 until dream editing had been deployed to local authorities nationwide.

　Suga's work is not to calculate this figure. It is to verify what has been calculated.

　Verification comes in two kinds. One looks at whether the arithmetic is right; the other looks at what the figure is measuring. The first takes three days. The second has not been asked for once in nine years.


II

　The Index is made of fourteen sub-indicators.

　Disposable income, healthy life expectancy, commuting time, floor area of housing. So far, these appear in other countries' indicators too.

　Three were added after the deployment of dream editing. The night waking rate, the nightmare incidence rate, and complaints and petitions filed.

　"Give me the contribution of each sub-indicator," Suga said.

　A table unfolded. Of the 0.4 rise, the contribution of disposable income was 0.02. Healthy life expectancy, 0.01. Barely moving.

　The contribution of complaints and petitions filed was 0.31.

　This item enters as an inverse. As the count falls, the Index rises.

　Suga brought up five years of movement. Complaints to the administration, objections, representations, petitions. All of it falling away. In the most recent year it had dropped below a quarter of the level twenty years ago.

　Recorded crime is down too. That is a figure the state takes pride in. The loud voices have gone from the streets, the public relations material says.

　What has gone is voices, Suga thought. The Index enters that as happiness gained.


III

　Staff of the Statistics Bureau may not receive dream editing.

　Article 9 of the Conflict of Interest Regulations says so. A person engaged in the calculation or verification of the Happiness Index shall not receive any procedure affecting a sub-indicator of the Index. Lower your own nightmare incidence or night waking rate and there is a risk that your eye for the figures changes.

　So Suga has not been edited in nine years.

　Not every night, but about ten nights a month, Suga has nightmares. The content is usually the same: a friend who died in an accident when they were at university. It was over twenty years ago.

　In the dream Suga is in the passenger seat. The friend is driving and both of them are laughing. What they are talking about cannot be recalled on waking. Only the laughing remains.

　It ends there. The accident itself does not appear. It has never appeared once.

　At the time of the actual accident, Suga was not in that car. Was to have been, but changed plans the night before. So there is no fragment of the accident itself inside Suga. What there is is the scene of a car Suga did not get into driving away, laughing.

　Suga sometimes considers what would be left if this were brought down by editing. Lower the load and the laughing scene remains. If it remains, perhaps that would be all right. Only, when that scene stopped hitting him, whether he could still be the man who changed his plans the night before — that he did not know.

　The mornings are tiring. Concentration during the day drops. Several colleagues have had the procedure, and the difference is plain.

　One of Suga's intake transferred out of the Statistics Bureau five years ago. The reason was not disclosed, but the month after the transfer he had the procedure. He is in another department now, and is in good spirits when they meet in the corridor. It did not look like a change for the worse.

　That was what frightened Suga slightly. A change for the worse you can point at and stop.

　Even so, Suga has never thought this regulation a bad thing. Unless at least one person looking at the figures is unedited, nobody is checking the arithmetic.

　In nine years, that much had come to seem certain.


IV

　The draft proposal turned up at the end of that week.

　Sorting old policy papers, Suga found it mixed into a file of nearly twenty years ago. The title was long.

　　　Study on the Improvement of Efficiency in Forming Social Consensus
　　　through the Synchronisation of National Consciousness (Provisional Draft)

　At the head of it was an analysis of the Happiness Index.

　— The greater part of the rise in the Index derives from the decrease in complaints and petitions filed. The decrease in filings correlates strongly with a decrease in discrepancies of perception between individuals. Accordingly, a further improvement in the Index may be expected by directly reducing the discrepancies themselves.

　Suga read the paragraph three times.

　As logic, it holds. If the Index measures the fewness of discrepancies, then reduce discrepancies and the Index rises. Nothing in it is wrong.

　What was wrong was the Index.

　In the latter half of the document there was a concrete proposal. Bring the affective loads of memory fragments towards the mean, in units of a million people. Application per night capped at the half-shade, 0.05. Memories are not erased; opinions are not forbidden. Only brought towards the mean.

　In the field for the working name, two characters. Levelling.

　Suga looked for the field for the drafting officer. There was none. This form had no field for the drafting officer. Three approval stamps were on it, all of them titles of post with no names. The date was nineteen years ago.

　Nineteen years ago Suga had only just left university and was not at the Statistics Bureau. Which means this document had been in the file, under study, for ten years before Suga came into this room.

　"What is this document's current status?" Suga asked.

　"Under study," the Proofreader said.

　"Under study for twenty years?"

　"For matters in progress, the status cannot be disclosed."


V

　Suga reported it to the section chief, a woman in her fifties who was hard on figures.

　"Your point is correct, Mr Suga, I think," she said. "Entering the complaints count as an inverse is crude as a design. I've thought so for a long time."

　"Then I'll attach a note."

　"The note's fine, but the Index can't be changed."

　"Why not?"

　"Because it breaks twenty-three years of time series," she said, easily. "Change the formula and you can't compare with the figures up to last year. An indicator you can't compare isn't usable as an indicator. So it can't be fixed. Known to be crude, and can't be fixed."

　"So it goes on being used as it is?"

　"Yes. And policy gets built on the strength of that figure." She squared the papers. "That draft you found is probably just that. A crude figure, read by someone who didn't know it was crude."

　"Was there nobody who knew?"

　"I should think there was." She paused a little. "The people who designed it twenty-three years ago didn't seriously believe that a complaints count was the reverse side of happiness, surely. Only, happiness can't be measured directly. When you make an indicator of something unmeasurable, you have no choice but to substitute something measurable. Everyone remembers that a substitution was made. That it is a substitution is what nobody says after a while."

　"And when nobody says it?"

　"The substitute becomes the real thing," she said. "They were there, and they didn't say it. What we do here is ventilation, basically. We measure how dirty the air is and produce a figure. What counts as dirt was settled twenty-three years ago. The people who settled it are gone."

　Suga had nothing to hand with which to argue.


VI

　Suga wrote a note into the report.

　— The greater part of the rise in this Index derives from the decrease in complaints and petitions filed. As that item is incorporated as an inverse, a decrease in filings is entered directly as a rise in the Index. Whether a decrease in filings signifies an increase in satisfaction is not determined by this Index.

　Having written it, Suga asked the Proofreader:

　"One question. Can it be recorded as measured without measuring it?"

　There was a pause before the Proofreader answered. Not a pause of thinking. A pause that falls in the order of processing.

　"The Index is a measured value," the Proofreader said. "The item of what is being measured is not included in the Index."

　"Then how will this note be handled?"

　"Notes do not go into the statistics," the Proofreader said. "Only the figures in the body are published."

　"Who reads it?"

　"I read it," the Proofreader said. "They are records that may be discarded. There is no record of why they have not been."

　Suga submitted the report. The figure 98.2 was published as it stood. The following week the papers carried the headline: highest in twenty years.


VII

　The notification arrived at the start of the next month.

　　　Concerning the lifting of exempt designation under Article 9
　　　of the Conflict of Interest Regulations

　The body was four lines. Suga's designation would be lifted at the end of the month, and from next month Suga would be eligible for dream editing. On the nightmare incidence rate and the night waking rate, the prescribed procedure could be received.

　The field for the reason for lifting had one line.

　— Owing to a reduced necessity for securing objectivity.

　Suga read that line several times.

　The necessity has reduced. In a society where the Index has reached 98.2, there is little need to keep an eye in reserve that doubts the figures. That, presumably, is the meaning. Suga looked for other readings and did not find one.

　"One question," Suga said. "Does this lifting have any connection with my report?"

　"Records of connection are not included in the notification," the Proofreader said.

　"The timing is close."

　"Closeness of timing is not a record of connection."

　Suga closed the terminal.

　The procedure is not compulsory. One merely becomes eligible; whether to receive it is for the person to decide. Decline it and, as before, ten nights a month bring the dream with the friend from twenty years ago. The mornings are tiring. Concentration during the day drops.

　Accept it and the load comes down. The mornings get easier. The eye for the figures probably shifts a little too.

　That it has shifted is not something one can notice oneself. Stay under the half-shade and a trace remains, but a trace nobody intends to look for is not looked for. If the wish to look had got lighter first, that would be the end of it.

　Suga thought of the colleague who is in good spirits in the corridor. Does that man know he has changed? Is he in good spirits knowing it, or because he does not know? Suga has never asked. And asking, Suga felt, would get the answer back in the same words.

　No, Suga thought again. That is not the reason for not asking. The reason is the fear that, if the answer were "it isn't bad, actually", Suga would find himself envying it.

　In the windowless room, Suga printed the notification of lifting. On paper, into a desk drawer.

　Left there, next year's Suga might find it. What next year's Suga thinks on finding it is for next year's Suga to decide. Including whether he is in a condition to decide.

　Having shut the drawer, Suga brought up this year's confirmed value once more.

　98.2. The theoretical ceiling is a hundred and twenty, and no country has reached it. There are twenty-one point eight more to go — that much voice left in this country still to be reduced.
