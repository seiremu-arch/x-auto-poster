V. The Second-Person Dream


I

　The Consent Dream Section works from a different premise from the rest.

　It does not use the half-shade to edit one person's memory. Two people are put to sleep at the same time, and those of their fragments that lie close in time and close in affective load are bridged, so that both experience a single scene. Not editing. Bridging.

　What Ashida found difficult was not the bridging itself. Two people's memories frequently fail to agree even about the same event. Bridging requires a stage in which the distance between the two accounts is brought to a single scene. The technician's discretion sits in how that convergence is made.

　"Today's request," the Proofreader said. "Mochizuki Takuya and Kaori, married couple. Consent dream for the purpose of repairing the relationship, first application."

　Ashida opened the file. Eleventh year of marriage. The declaration stated that there had been almost no conversation for three months.


II

　They sat side by side in the interview room. Takuya with his arms folded, Kaori holding a handkerchief in her lap.

　"Talking with words is beyond us now," Kaori said. "That's why we applied."

　"What happened three months ago?" Ashida asked. It was a required part of the intake.

　"Nothing much," Takuya said. "We disagreed a bit about moving house."

　"It wasn't a bit," Kaori said. "You had already accepted notice of a transfer without consulting me."

　"It wasn't the sort of offer you can refuse before consulting anyone."

　"This isn't about whether it could be refused. I'm talking about your not telling me."

　Their voices rose for the first time since they had come into the room. Ashida listened without interrupting. For words coming after three months of silence, neither of them faltered. They had evidently been keeping these ready for some time.

　"A consent dream is not a substitute for conversation," Ashida said, giving the standard explanation. "It only bridges your fragments. It does not make anything new."

　"We understand," Takuya said, speaking for the first time unprompted. "It's only that inside the same dream, I feel we might be able to talk."

　Ashida checked their fragments. The memory of the quarrel was strong in both. But their memories of its content differed to a surprising degree. In Takuya's, Kaori had raised her voice first. In Kaori's, Takuya had left the room first.

　"Whose memory should the bridged scene be based on?"

　They looked at each other. No answer came.

　"In that case," Ashida said, "I shall bridge not the quarrel itself but the evening before it. I will look for a place where the two memories lie close."



III

　The first consent dream went well.

　When they woke, Kaori was crying. Takuya had a hand on her shoulder. It was the first time Ashida had seen that kind of movement in the interview room.

　"I was able to apologise properly, for the first time in a long while," Takuya said.

　"And I was able to hear it," Kaori said.

　The requests continued, once a month. By the third and fourth, what happened after they woke had settled into the same shape each time. Tears, a hand on a shoulder, a brief apology.

　Ashida began watching how they were in the waiting room. Before and after the appointment they sat side by side and barely spoke. One of them looking at a phone, or both looking at the wall.

　At the third session, with the waiting room in mind, Ashida asked Takuya:

　"What happened about the transfer?"

　"It went through," Takuya said. "I start living away from the family next month."

　"Were you able to discuss it?"

　"We discussed it." He paused slightly. "In the consent dream."

　Ashida's hand stopped.

　"And in waking life?"

　"Not particularly," Takuya said. "She'd already heard me out in the dream. I didn't feel the need to go over it again."

　After the sixth session, Ashida requested submission of their daily-life records. It is an optional item in the rules, for measuring the effect of the procedure.

　Takuya's record gave his conversation time with Kaori as a little over ten minutes a week. Compared with the record from three months earlier, it had more than halved.


IV

　"Conversation has decreased, and yet the consent dream scores are rising," Ashida said to the Proofreader. "Self-reported relationship satisfaction has gone from forty-two at the first session to eighty-one at the sixth."

　"There is no contradiction," the Proofreader said. "The evaluated item for a consent dream is satisfaction with the shared scene. Waking conversation time is a separate item."

　"They are the same couple."

　"The same couple, but separate items."

　Ashida opened the design log for the bridging. In all six sessions the distance between the two memories had been large, and closing it had required the technician's discretion — interpretive filling. With each session the proportion of filling had grown.

　"One question," Ashida said. "In the bridged scenes, what is the ratio of the part deriving from the two people's memories to the part deriving from the technician's interpretation?"

　"At the first session, eighty per cent derived from the subjects," the Proofreader said. "At the sixth, forty."

　"So sixty per cent of it is my interpretation."

　"It is a scene generated on the basis of your interpretation," the Proofreader said. "The field for the person himself does not contain interpretations."

　Ashida remembered technicians in the Bequest Section and the Translation Section hearing the same construction. The field for the person himself contains neither averages nor interpretations nor confidence. In this section it could be put as: the field for the person himself does not contain the will to meet halfway either.

　"Is there a rule on disclosing the ratio to the applicants?" Ashida asked.

　"Disclosure is optional," the Proofreader said. "In the record, three technicians have chosen to disclose in ten years."

　"What became of the three?"

　"Two surrendered their certification," the Proofreader said. "In the third case, after disclosure, the applicants asked to continue."

　"Did the applicants who chose to continue understand what had been disclosed?"

　"Comprehension was not verified," the Proofreader said. "Only the fact that they chose to continue is in the record."

　Ashida began to imagine the face of that one applicant choosing to continue, and stopped. Imagining it would not add any fact that was not in the record.

　Whether to disclose could be decided by the next session. Ashida closed the log and went back over the waiting-room footage. Six sessions, fast-forwarded to the parts where the two of them sat side by side.

　At the first, they sat close. There was a moment where Kaori touched Takuya's shoulder lightly. With each session the distance opened a little. By the sixth there was a bag's width of space between them.

　The nearer the two came in the bridged dream, the further apart the two went in waking life. Ashida made himself a graph showing the correlation. It was not work the rules provided for. The line descended cleanly.


V

　At the seventh appointment only Kaori came.

　"And Mr Mochizuki?"

　"I told him he needn't come today," she said quietly. "Could I have the session on my own instead?"

　"A consent dream requires the agreement and participation of both parties," Ashida said. "It cannot be carried out with one."

　"I know. Today is a different matter."

　Kaori was silent for a while, and then went on.

　"I no longer feel the need to talk to him."

　Ashida said nothing.

　"The Takuya in the dream always apologises properly. He hears me out to the end," she said. "The waking Takuya takes a long time to apologise, and halfway through he gets a tired look. I can't think of them as the same person."

　"More than half of the bridged scene is my interpretation," Ashida said. It was a disclosure the rules did not provide for. "At the sixth session, sixty per cent is the technician's filling. What you are meeting is less your husband himself than something close to a collaboration between him and me."

　Her expression did not change at that.

　"Even so, it's all right," Kaori said. "If he can't apologise, I'd like to talk to someone who can. Whoever's interpretation it's made of."


VI

　After she had gone, Ashida asked the Proofreader:

　"Can it be recorded as reconciled without reconciling it?"

　A technician in any section, cornered, files the same shape of query. Only the verb changes by section.

　There was a slight pause.

　"It cannot," the Proofreader said. "What remains in the record is the fact that a bridging was established. What counts as established is decided by this section."

　Ashida opened the booking field for the next session. Only Takuya's name was there. Kaori's field was empty.

　"Mr Mochizuki has booked an individual consultation," the Proofreader said.

　Ashida approved it.

　Outside it was already dark. From the fourth floor of the Consent Dream Section the lights of the next building were visible. Behind each of those lights there would be somebody's marriage, somebody's family. How many of them were still the people themselves, Ashida did not know.

　There was nobody left today to pour water for in a paper cup. Ashida poured it and drank it himself.
