IX. The Right Not to Wake


I

　The corridor of the Long Sleep wing is about two degrees warmer than the other floors.

　Forty beds, in two rows. They are not all occupied. In room 31, which is Arimura's, Kiuchi Toshi has been for three years and two months. Eighty-seven years old.

　The Long Sleep means a procedure in which sleep is medically extended and synthesised fragments are replayed continuously. In law it falls under a classification of end-of-life care. The body is maintained by drip and by turning. Asleep, the person sleeps on until the death is attended.

　Arimura's work was to go round these beds once a month.

　"Room 31, welfare check," Arimura said, opening the terminal.

　"Survival confirmed," the Proofreader said. "Respiration and circulation are both under the physician's management. Fragment replay is also continuing."

　Bands of colour lined up on the monitor. There is nothing visible but the bands. No device for rendering the contents as a picture has been built anywhere in forty years. What Kiuchi Toshi is seeing now, Arimura does not know.

　Nor is there any need to know, the rules say.

　Of the forty beds, twenty-six were occupied. An empty bed does not mean a discharge. There are only two ways out of the Long Sleep wing, waking or the attended death. In eight years Arimura had attended nine wakings and over a hundred and forty deaths.

　At a death the family sometimes come and sometimes do not. When they do not, Arimura attends at the end. At those times Arimura sits in the chair beside the bed and watches the load bands for a while. The contents are not visible. Only what colour the last band was goes into the record.


II

　There are two items to check. Survival, and the continuation of consent.

　Survival is the physician's. What Arimura checks is the consent.

　The Long Sleep requires the person's prior consent. Kiuchi Toshi signed for herself three years and two months ago. But consent is not valid in perpetuity from the moment of starting. The scheme requires a monthly check of whether the consent is continuing.

　You cannot verify consent in words from a sleeping person.

　So the scheme looks at the load.

　"Room 31, mean load over the last thirty days."

　"0.19," the Proofreader said. "The satisfaction range is 0.10 to 0.35. Within range."

　Within range, consent is deemed to be continuing. Outside it, the case goes to review.

　Arimura had been unable to swallow this provision since first reading it. A low load means only that the memory is not hitting the person. It does not mean that she is satisfied.

　Even so, there was no other method of checking.

　When the scheme began, the continuation of consent was confirmed by a monthly visit from the family. The family would see how the person was and declare whether to continue.

　In the fourteenth year the method changed. The family attendance rate had fallen below thirty per cent. A scheme that has relatives who do not come make the judgement cannot stand. So it was replaced by the load determination.

　The field for the reason for the amendment reads: to improve objectivity. However many times Arimura read that line, it would not read that way.


III

　Kiuchi Toshi's dream is a synthesis.

　Her husband, Kiuchi Shoichi, died twelve years ago. From data from his lifetime — photographs, recordings, interviews with the family — a technician in the Bequest Section assembled the fragments. The confidence of the synthesis, according to the record, was sixty-four per cent. The remainder is the technician's interpretation.

　For three years and two months, Kiuchi Toshi has been living with that husband.

　"One question," Arimura said. "Give me the load over the three years."

　A line appeared. It was almost flat. For three years it had swayed gently between 0.18 and 0.22.

　In one place only, the line jumped.

　Six months ago. In one night it rose to 0.71, and over three days it came back to 0.20.

　"What is this?"

　"An anomalous value," the Proofreader said. "It was referred to review after occurrence, in accordance with the rules."

　"And the outcome?"

　"It was processed as a value that may be ignored," the Proofreader said. "Because it returned within range inside three days."

　Arimura opened the record for that day. The replay log for the synthesised fragments was there. A technician cannot see the contents, but the kinds of component in the fragments do go into the record.

　That night's log stated that the replay of the husband's fragments had ended partway through, and that they had not been replayed since.

　Inside the synthesised dream, Kiuchi Shoichi had died six months ago.


IV

　Arimura read the record through three times.

　A synthesised fragment has a lifespan set for it. Make a husband who goes on living indefinitely and the divergence from the person's grasp of reality grows too wide. By design, the Bequest Section sets an age of death. The age set for Kiuchi Shoichi was eighty-four.

　Kiuchi Toshi had seen her husband out a second time, inside the dream.

　The figure 0.71 was that night's grief. Its return to 0.20 over three days was not because the grief had gone. It is only that people stop weeping after three days.

　The scheme determined those three days to be a value that may be ignored. Because it came back within range.

　"One question," Arimura said. "Can it be recorded as consented to without her having consented?"

　The Proofreader's answer came half a beat later than usual.

　"Consent is not recorded directly as an item," the Proofreader said. "What is recorded is the load. The rules provide that consent is deemed to be continuing by virtue of the load being within range."

　"Deemed, meaning?"

　"Meaning that whether it is in fact so has not been checked."


V

　The son came that Friday.

　Kiuchi Yasutaka, sixty-one. In three years and two months there were only four visits on record.

　"Could you wake her?"

　He said it in the interview room as soon as he sat down. Before producing the form, Arimura decided to listen first.

　"May I ask your reasons?"

　"A grandchild has been born," he said. "A great-grandchild, for my mother."

　"Congratulations."

　"It isn't only that." He folded his hands on his knees. "When my mother came in here, I opposed it. I said terrible things. What's the pleasure in living with a fake of Dad, I said."

　Arimura listened without speaking.

　"My mother didn't answer back. She signed, and by the next week she was asleep," he said. "Three years, like that. I still haven't apologised for what I said then."

　"An application to wake her can be made by the family," Arimura said. "There are some things I should tell you first."

　In the physician's assessment, Kiuchi Toshi's body had visibly declined over the past year. Continued in the Long Sleep, a few more months. Woken, and if weaning off tube feeding went well, a possibility of two to three years.

　Waking, however, puts a physical load on the body. At eighty-seven the success rate was around sixty per cent.

　"Sixty per cent."

　"Sixty per cent."

　He was silent for a long time.

　"And my mother's wishes?"

　"Consent is deemed to be continuing," Arimura said. "Because the load is in the satisfaction range."

　"So my mother is happy."

　At that, Arimura could not find an answer.


VI

　Answering by the rules, there was only one answer. The load is within range, therefore consent is continuing, therefore Kiuchi Toshi goes on wishing for the Long Sleep.

　Arimura told him about the jump six months ago. It was a disclosure the rules did not provide for.

　"Your mother has seen your father out a second time, inside the dream."

　He looked up.

　"Six months ago. A synthesised fragment has a lifespan set by the design. He was set to die at eighty-four," Arimura said. "That night the load rose to 0.71. It came back in three days."

　"Came back, meaning?"

　"It means she stopped weeping, I think," Arimura said. "The scheme processed it as a value that may be ignored. Because it returned within range."

　He opened his mouth, and closed it without saying anything.

　"Is my mother alone in there now?"

　"I don't know," Arimura said. "All I can see is the bands. I cannot see the contents. Only that the husband's fragments have not been replayed since six months ago."

　He covered his face with both hands and stayed like that for a while.

　Arimura poured water into a paper cup and set it on the desk. Eight years in this work have taught one thing. When someone is crying, the only thing a technician can do is set down the water.


VII

　The application went up to the facility director the same day.

　The director was a man in his sixties. He spoke while stamping the approval.

　"Good judgement, Mr Arimura. The family come, they decide, she is woken. That's the ideal shape of it."

　"The success rate is sixty per cent."

　"Sixty is on the high side, at eighty-seven," the director said. "What we do here is ventilation, basically. You open a closed room and let the air through. What flies out when you open it isn't ours to decide."

　"So there is something that flies out."

　"Three years' worth of something, yes." He set the stamp down. "But leaving it shut is a decision too. It's only deciding by not opening."

　Arimura did nod at that. With even one place to nod at, this work becomes a little easier.

　The waking was carried out the following Tuesday.

　The applicant was the son; the approvals came from the physician and the director. Arimura, responsible for the attendance record, was in the corner of the room.

　The waking took four hours. Kiuchi Toshi opened her eyes a little after three in the afternoon.

　At first her gaze wandered over the ceiling. Then she saw her son, standing beside the bed.

　"You," Kiuchi Toshi said. Her voice was hoarse. "You came."

　He took his mother's hand.

　"Mum, look, I'm — I'm sorry about everything."

　Whether she was hearing it or not, Kiuchi Toshi looked towards the ceiling for a while. Then, as though remembering, she said:

　"Your father, last year —"

　His hand stopped.

　"Last year, he died," Kiuchi Toshi said. "You were busy, you couldn't come, could you. It's all right. I was with him to the end."

　He said nothing.

　His father died twelve years ago. All three people in the room know it.

　"I see," he said. "I see."

　"He had a good face on him," Kiuchi Toshi said. "At the end. Properly."

　Still holding his mother's hand, he looked once towards Arimura. It was the look of someone asking whether to correct her.

　Arimura shook his head. It was an instruction the rules did not provide for. Arimura has no authority to decide whether a correction is made. Only, Arimura could not think what correcting it now would obtain.

　"I see." He turned back to his mother. "I'm sorry, Mum. For not getting there."

　"It's all right," Kiuchi Toshi said. "You're a busy man."

　Three years and two months' worth of apology was not spent there. There was no longer anywhere to spend it.

　Arimura looked down at the record sheet.

　There is a field for the condition after waking. Four options. Lucid, mild confusion, moderate confusion, severe confusion.

　Arimura marked mild confusion.

　Having marked it, Arimura set the pen down for a while.

　This person is not confused. Over three years and two months this person lived with her husband again, saw him out again, and stopped weeping after three days. She remembers it in exactly that order.

　Which of the two twelve-year spans is the true one is not for Arimura to decide. Nor for the scheme. If it could be decided at all, it would be only by the number of people who remember it together. In this room it was two to one.

　There were only four fields that could be written in.

　Outside the window it was bright. It is about two degrees colder here than in the Long Sleep corridor. Still holding her son's hand, Kiuchi Toshi was drifting back to sleep. This time, nobody was replaying any fragments.
