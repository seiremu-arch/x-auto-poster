I. The Sleepless City


I

　It is seven years since his body stopped needing sleep.

　Not needing it, strictly, is not the case. Three hours came to be enough, that is all. Even so, set against seven years ago, it works out that life has become about ten per cent longer.

　Ban worked for the company that sells that ten per cent.

　The Dream Sales Section; formally, the Dream Experience Product Development Section. Into the forty minutes or so of the three hours' sleep that corresponds to REM, designed fragments are poured. Time spent sleeping is turned into time spent consuming a product.

　"Today's distribution, seven million two hundred thousand," the Proofreader read out. "By composition: travel line forty per cent, achievement line thirty, interpersonal twenty, the remainder unclassified."

　Ban's responsibility was the achievement line. Passing. Completing. Being recognised. The load is adjusted so that the whole thing can be experienced in forty minutes and only the satisfaction is left by morning.

　People no longer have to sleep. In exchange, they have begun to buy sleeping.

　Seven years ago, when the sleep-reduction procedure came under insurance, the papers wrote that working hours would fall. What actually fell was sleeping hours. The five hours freed up became, directly, hours spent awake.

　Ban has no memory of anyone making an issue of it. There may have been voices that did; they did not reach Ban's ears. That they did not reach him is a thing Ban has lately begun to mind a little.


II

　The product fragments are syntheses.

　Rather than editing an actual memory, new fragments are assembled from existing data. As a technique it is the Bequest Section's; only the purpose differs. They reproduce the dead; here, things that did not happen are made.

　Synthesis requires material.

　"This term's materials procurement cost," Ban said, opening the terminal.

　"Four point two billion yen," the Proofreader said. "Up eleven per cent on last term."

　The quality of a synthesised fragment is decided by the quality of the material. Records of affect a person actually felt are needed. You cannot make elation out of nothing.

　There are two sources of supply. One is subjects who have consented to provide, and the unit price is high. The other is users of the free tier.

　The free tier gives free treatment of nightmares on condition of dreaming with advertising in it. Article 11 of the contract includes consent to the statistical use of fragments obtained in the course of the procedure. The scope of the phrase statistical use is not written into the article.

　"The procurement ratio from the free tier?"

　"Eighty-three per cent of all material."


III

　What gave Ban pause came at the April product trial.

　The new product was a premium version of the achievement line. Forty minutes in which long effort is repaid. Evaluations at the prototype stage were high, and in internal trials satisfaction was over ninety.

　Ban tried it too. When he woke, his cheeks were wet.

　As a product, that was the correct response. The design targets say induction of a lacrimal response. What was written was what came out.

　Only, what Ban experienced in those forty minutes was not his own life.

　A synthesised fragment is made by averaging the affect of several people. So it does not become any particular person's experience. It should not.

　What Ban saw was a factory. A passage, bitterly cold, at the end of a night shift. Somebody calls his name and hands him a document. Ban did not know what the document meant. Not knowing, the exact way his chest tightened at the moment of taking it was distinct.

　Ban has never worked in a factory. He has never worked a night shift. But the cold of the passage had the concreteness of something the soles of his feet remembered.

　After waking, Ban sat on the bed for half an hour. The feeling arrived first and his head went looking afterwards for a memory to fit it. Finding none, his head offered up something near instead. The posting of the university entrance results. The delivery date of the first project he was given. None of them matched. He could tell they did not match, and the way his chest tightened was the same.

　In the observations field of the trial report, Ban wrote only: satisfactory. It was the only field there was to write in.


IV

　"Can the affect of a single provider remain in a synthesised fragment?"

　"By design it cannot," the Proofreader said. "A synthesis averages the affect of at least two hundred people. Given the nature of an average, a single experience remaining in identifiable form is held to be unlikely."

　"Unlikely, meaning?"

　"Meaning there is no record of its not occurring."

　Ban opened the breakdown of the material. The fragments used in this product came from two hundred and thirty-one people. Of those, a hundred and ninety-two were free-tier users.

　Open the breakdown further and a table of contribution weights came up. The two hundred and thirty-one do not tell equally. The higher a fragment's affective load, the harder it pulls the average.

　The top contributor's weight was nineteen per cent.

　"Is this an average?"

　"It is an average," the Proofreader said. "A weighted average."

　Hear that it is the average of two hundred and thirty-one people and it sounds like affect belonging to nobody. In fact nearly a fifth of it belonged to one person. Who that one is appears only as an identifier.

　Ban looked at the contribution field for a while. From next month this product goes out to seven hundred thousand people.


V

　Ban submitted an enquiry on the top provider's identifier.

　"That cannot be disclosed," the Proofreader said. "The identity of a material provider cannot be consulted in the course of product development."

　"Can you give me the circumstances of the provision alone?"

　"I can."

　According to the record, the fragment had been harvested two years earlier. It had been obtained incidentally in the course of a free-tier nightmare procedure. The target of the procedure was a different fragment; this affect had merely been next to it.

　Its classification on harvest was high load, high purity. As product material, the top grade.

　"Does the person know this became a product?"

　"There is no provision for notification," the Proofreader said. "Individual notification is not included in the Article 11 consent."

　"The consideration?"

　"The cost of the free-tier procedure constitutes the consideration."

　Ban worked it out. The unit cost of a free-tier procedure was a few hundred yen. This product sells at eight hundred yen and goes to seven hundred thousand people.

　The difference in magnitude did not need a calculator.

　"One question," Ban said. "Is there any mechanism for paying this provider further consideration?"

　"There is not," the Proofreader said. "The materials procurement cost is fixed at the point of harvest."

　"It does not change if the product sells?"

　"It does not. The value of material is set by its grading at harvest and is not linked to sales performance."

　"What sets the grading?"

　"Affective load and purity," the Proofreader said. "The higher a fragment's load, the higher it is graded."

　Ban considered what that meant for a while.

　The more a person has suffered, the better the material. Being better material changes nothing about the consideration. A high load means the person felt that deeply, and the reason for feeling deeply is usually that their life has not been easy.

　Going back to the record of two years ago, there was one line in the remarks field at harvest.

　— Procedure on target fragment divided over three nights. Reason for division: avoidance of drag on the adjacent fragment.

　Which means there was a technician who divided it over three nights. Somebody who did not finish it in one, and took three so as not to damage what was beside it. That technician will not have known either that the affect lying beside it would be graded top and made into a product.


VI

　He took it to the section chief that Friday. A man in his forties, strong on figures.

　"Mr Ban, legal has signed off on that."

　"I know."

　"Statistical use in Article 11 is the clause that exists for exactly this. The average of two hundred and thirty-one people isn't any one person's. That's legal's view."

　"The top contributor is nineteen per cent."

　"Nineteen isn't a majority, is it." He said it easily. "Over fifty-one and we pull it. That's our internal rule."

　"Where does the fifty come from?"

　"Same place as a vote," the chief said. "Over half and it's that person's. Under, it's everyone's. What we do here is ventilation, basically. We collect the air out of people's rooms, mix it, and send it round the city. Who the mixture belongs to stopped being decidable the moment it was mixed."

　Ban looked for the place where the reasoning was wrong. He did not find it.

　Not finding it and being satisfied were two different things.

　"One thing, if I may," Ban said. "Do you buy our products?"

　The chief looked slightly surprised. Then he answered honestly.

　"I do. Two or three a month."

　"Which?"

　"Interpersonal. Not our section's." He said it plainly. "The forty minutes where you talk properly with your family. My family don't actually say much to each other."

　Ban had nothing to give back to that.

　"Mr Ban, this isn't a bad thing," the chief went on. "That people like me are saved by it is a fact. It's only that I make a point of not thinking about where those forty minutes came from. Think about it and you can't buy it."

　"So you have thought about it."

　"Once," the chief said. "The year I joined. I said what you've just said and got the answer you've just had."


VII

　The product was distributed on schedule.

　First-month sales exceeded the internal projection by thirty per cent. Satisfaction, ninety-one. Returns, 0.2 per cent. Every figure was good.

　Ban read the purchasers' reviews.

　— I cried. It was like remembering the happiest day of my life.

　— I remembered what it feels like to have something repaid.

　— The afterglow lasted until morning. Glad I bought it.

　Several of them wrote like remembering. They did not remember. They borrowed, for forty minutes, affect that was not theirs. But the borrowing does not go into any record, and there is nowhere to return it.

　Ban thought about the factory passage. Bitterly cold, at the end of a night shift.

　Is that person still a free-tier user? Shown advertising each time they sleep, having the weight of their nightmares brought down, not knowing that the top-graded affect harvested in the process is being sold to seven hundred thousand people at eight hundred yen.

　"One question," Ban said. "Can it be recorded as blended without blending it?"

　There was a pause before the Proofreader answered.

　"It cannot," the Proofreader said. "What remains in the record is the number of material items procured and the category of the source. There is no field for writing whose contributed how much."


VIII

　Ban filed one submission through the internal suggestion scheme.

　It proposed a mechanism for notifying top contributors that their fragment had been made into a product. He did not go as far as a review of the consideration. He knew it would not get through if he did.

　The answer came three weeks later.

　— Notification is deferred at this time, as it risks giving rise to unnecessary expectations on the part of providers.

　Ban read the line and closed the terminal.

　Unnecessary expectations. What it would be awkward to be expected to do is not written. It was presumably too obvious to need writing.

　That night Ban bought one of his company's products. The budget version of the achievement line, four hundred yen. For forty minutes of three hours' sleep, Ban borrowed somebody else's repaid moment.

　In the morning, only the satisfaction remained. As designed.

　Whose, and what, he had borrowed, Ban did not know either. There was no field for it anywhere in the purchase history.
