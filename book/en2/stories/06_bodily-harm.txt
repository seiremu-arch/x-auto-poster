VI. Bodily Harm


I

　The victim is alive.

　He can give a statement and he can walk home. There is no external injury. The blood tests show nothing.

　Even so the complaint was accepted. The charge is bodily harm.

　"Confirming the record of interview," Tadenuma said, opening the terminal. He is with the National Police Agency's Dream Offences Unit. Six years since it was set up.

　"Victim, Kuwashima Keiichi, forty-nine," the Proofreader read out. "The complaint is of a deliberate raising of the affective load over seven nights."

　"His own account?"

　"He states that he began having the same dream every night; that it grew harder by the day; and that on the eighth day he could not get to work."

　Tadenuma looked at the figures. The load on the target fragment had risen over seven days from 0.12 to 0.47.

　0.05 a night. Exactly the half-shade.


II

　Dream editing is a technique for lowering.

　Operating in the upward direction is permitted in law in one setting only, correctional treatment. Any other upward application is unlawful. Unlawful, but not technically difficult. The only difference is pushing or pulling.

　What is difficult is doing it to another person.

　"Where was the device installed?"

　"In the ceiling void of the victim's bedroom at home," the Proofreader said. "One small application device was recovered. Its output is about a quarter of the professional type."

　"Does a quarter produce the half-shade?"

　"It does, at close range."

　Tadenuma looked at the line for a while.

　Professional devices are installed in the Bureau's editing rooms and handled by certified technicians. What was used here was a modified commercial sleep monitor. The circuit diagram has been public for four years.

　It was published by a patients' organisation for sleep disorders. The intent was to let people who cannot afford the device, and so cannot receive treatment, build one within the bounds of personal use.

　For personal use only, says a note on the diagram. A note has no force.

　The Unit has three times requested that publication be stopped. All three times the organisation refused. The reason given was the same each time: without this there are people who cannot get treatment.

　Tadenuma could not say that the claim was wrong either.


III

　The suspect was the victim's elder brother.

　Kuwashima Koji, fifty-three. The motive field says: long-standing conflict over an inheritance.

　When Tadenuma attended the interview, Kuwashima Koji admitted it from the start.

　"I did it."

　"What did you do?"

　"Made my brother's dream heavier."

　The account was level. It could be read as showing remorse or as showing none.

　"How did you select that fragment?"

　"The day of our father's funeral," Koji said. "My brother didn't cry that day. I did. For four years it wouldn't leave my head."

　"And you made that memory heavier."

　"I wanted him to carry it at the same weight," Koji said. "I handed him in seven days what I'd been carrying for four years, that's all."

　Tadenuma's hand stopped on the record.

　There is as yet no provision for how to enter this account.

　The motive classification table has fourteen categories. Money. Grudge. Infatuation. Retaliation. Control. Other. It looked as though it fitted all of them and it settled in none.

　Is wanting his brother to carry it at the same weight retaliation? Handing over in seven days what he had carried for four years sounds less like harm than like distribution. There is no category of distribution in the table.

　"One question," Tadenuma said. "Is there a motive category for the purpose of sharing?"

　"There is not," the Proofreader said.

　"Then under what category are past cases of this kind processed?"

　"Retaliation is the most frequent," the Proofreader said. "Two hundred and thirty of four hundred and eleven. Then control, ninety. Other, sixty-one."

　"What is in the other?"

　"It is not tabulated."


IV

　Dream offences are an area the law has not caught up with.

　When the Unit was formed six years ago, what was anticipated was unauthorised editing. Making another person's dream lighter without their consent. The concern was its use as a means of fraud or of destroying evidence.

　What actually happened was the reverse.

　"Recorded cases of unauthorised upward application?"

　"Four hundred and eleven this year," the Proofreader said. "Six years ago it was nine."

　"Unauthorised downward editing?"

　"Twenty-three this year."

　People do not put a device in the ceiling void to make another person's memory lighter. They put it there to make it heavier.

　Debate on the statutory penalty continues. Where bodily harm is charged, how the degree of harm is to be measured becomes the question. The size of the rise in load, the interference with daily life, or the duration. In each case there is too little precedent.

　"The going rate in sentencing?"

　"It is not established," the Proofreader said. "There are several cases of immediate custody for a rise of 0.3 or more, and several in which the sentence was suspended."


V

　Tadenuma met the victim, Kuwashima Keiichi, once.

　It was to interview him as a witness. Keiichi looked tired, but spoke steadily.

　"Do you want your brother punished?" Tadenuma asked. It was not a question the rules provided for.

　Keiichi was quiet for a while.

　"I don't know."

　"Don't know, meaning?"

　"I didn't cry at our father's funeral," Keiichi said. "That is a fact. And all this time I felt nothing about it."

　"And now?"

　"Now I do," Keiichi said. "I know it is something my brother put there. Knowing it, I feel it all the same."

　Tadenuma wrote that into the record.

　Writing it, he considered how the statement would work on sentencing. It is evidence that the harm is serious, and it is evidence that the suspect's purpose was achieved. One and the same sentence reads both ways.

　"It can be put back, can't it," Keiichi asked.

　"It can," Tadenuma said. "A procedure lowering what was raised is available at public expense as victim restoration. It can only come down by the half-shade, though, so it takes seven nights or more."

　"If it's lowered, will I go back to feeling nothing about it?"

　"That is what happens."

　Keiichi thought for a while.

　"Then I won't have it."

　"Without it, the symptoms continue," Tadenuma said. "The quality of your sleep will not recover either."

　"I understand."

　"If you don't mind, may I ask your reason?"

　Keiichi looked for the words a little.

　"I've lived forty-nine years feeling nothing about my father," Keiichi said. "I think my brother could never forgive that in me. What he did is a crime. There's no question of it. But if I erase what my brother put there, I go back to being the man I was."

　"Is going back a bad thing?"

　"It isn't bad," Keiichi said. "Only, the man I was is a man who didn't cry at his father's funeral. A man who feels nothing about that. Having a procedure in order to go back to being him is — how shall I say."

　He broke off there.

　"I can't put it well."

　Tadenuma wrote that into the record too. He wrote it down to the line I can't put it well.


VI

　The decision affected the investigation.

　If the restoration procedure is not received, the state of harm is held to be continuing. Continuing, the degree of bodily harm is assessed more seriously. The prosecution naturally argues it.

　The defence pressed the point that the victim is refusing the procedure. He is maintaining a recoverable condition by his own choice, so part of the loss is attributable to the victim himself — that was the construction.

　There was a case in both.

　"One question," Tadenuma said. "Is there a field for recording the victim's reason for refusing?"

　"There is," the Proofreader said. "The field for reason for refusal of victim restoration. Four options."

　"Read them."

　"Medical contraindication. Economic circumstances. Religious grounds. Other."

　Tadenuma selected Other. In the free-text field he wrote Keiichi's words.

　— I know it is something my brother put there. Knowing it, I feel it all the same.

　"Where is this entry consulted?"

　"It is not included in the sentencing material," the Proofreader said. "Only the category of refusal is consulted."

　"So only the category Other is conveyed."

　"That is so."

　Tadenuma imagined how that field would be read in court. Not a medical contraindication, not economic circumstances, not religious grounds. Other. A word carrying no more meaning than that the reason did not fit into four types.

　The prosecution will read it as evidence that the victim is neglecting his recovery. The defence will read it as the victim having particular circumstances. Both will be reading something that is not written.

　"One question," Tadenuma said. "Why does the free-text field exist?"

　"Because there is a provision requiring the entry," the Proofreader said.

　"You require an entry that is not consulted?"

　"That is what the provision says," the Proofreader said. "On whether it is consulted, the provision is silent."


VII

　The verdict was guilty, with the sentence suspended.

　The reasons for sentence cited that the rise was limited to 0.35, that the victim had no organic damage, and that the suspect had admitted the offence in full. On the victim's refusal of the procedure, both parties' arguments were set out side by side and no finding was made.

　Three months after the verdict, Tadenuma was closing the case file.

　Kuwashima Keiichi's restoration procedure remained unperformed.

　Tadenuma looked at the field for a while.

　In this case the load went from 0.12 to 0.47. Something amounting to 0.35 increased inside Kuwashima Keiichi. His brother increased it, and that was found to be a crime.

　But the content of what increased is remorse at not having cried at his father's funeral.

　That may be something that could well have been there to begin with. Something that could well have been there was put there by criminal means. The one it was put into has chosen to go on holding it.

　"One question," Tadenuma said. "Is there a method of distinguishing what was raised from what a person came to hold themselves?"

　"There is not," the Proofreader said. "No record of derivation attaches to a fragment."

　"Then the victim's remorse —"

　"There is no field for remorse," the Proofreader said. "What is recorded is the value of the load and the history of its variation."

　The variation history keeps seven nights of rising. Who raised it is in the device seizure record. Set the two against each other and the fact of the crime can be reconstructed.

　What cannot be reconstructed is whether the remorse became Kuwashima Keiichi's own.

　Tadenuma closed the file and opened the next case. The four hundred and eleventh this year.
