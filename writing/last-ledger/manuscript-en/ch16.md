# Chapter Sixteen: The Fourth Time

The estate account was opened on February 24.

At the Ugano branch of Joshu Akagi Bank. The account name was *Estate of the late Soichiro Tono — Executor Shuji Kamiya*. A passbook and a registered seal, no cash card. The registered seal was a new one Kamiya had had made.

On March 4, there was seventy-two million yen in that account.

He had closed the deposits, sold the shares, and put everything together in one place for the time being. The contract with the trust bank was not yet signed. The papers would be ready in mid-March and the trust set up after that. For those two weeks or so, seventy-two million sat in an account with Kamiya's name on it.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

That night Kamiya was upstairs in the six-mat room of the house in Takasaki.

It was past eleven. He laid out on the desk the passbook, the registered seal, and a calculator. He laid them out to reconcile the figures in the schedule. That was what he told himself.

He looked at the balance column.

　　72,043,518

He had looked at other people's money until he was sick of it. So he was used to it. He thought he was used to it.

It was the first time he had seen seventy-two million in a passbook with his own name printed on it.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Not thinking about something is impossible.

Trying not to think is itself thinking. For thirty years Kamiya had seen it in people's faces. The look a borrower gives for an instant, on the other side of the counter.

The look he was wearing now.

He pulled the calculator toward him.

Twenty-eight million.

What had vanished into Arclight Capital. If that amount came back tomorrow, he would not have to tell Kayoko. He could give Mai the eight million for her deposit. He had said the fixed deposit matured the year after next. Fill the hole before then, and the lie would end as a lie, and nothing more.

Seventy-two million minus twenty-eight million was forty-four million.

The trust was being set up in mid-March. If the balance did not match when it was set up, it would show. So it would have to go back before then. Two weeks was impossible.

Kamiya pressed the keys.

Once.

He thought about how to delay the trust. A defect in the paperwork could push the contract with the trust bank back a month. If the executor delayed it, no one would suspect. A month and a half — no, even a month and a half would not be enough to put it back.

Twice.

He worked out what he could put back. Sell the house, eighteen million. What was left of the retirement money, four million. A loan against the pension, two million. Not enough.

Three times.

He thought about the simplest version. Don't put it back. When the trust was set up, report that the cash had been forty-four million. The evidence, he would make himself. The executor draws up the schedule. Tachibana would look at the schedule, but the executor obtains the bank balance certificates. The executor reports the proceeds of the share sales.

Who would notice?

Ryosuke would not. He did not look at figures. Chizuru read documents, but she did not know what her father's assets had been. Misaki only looked at the company's numbers. Tachibana would not suspect.

There was Soichiro's note. *Land one hundred million, business one hundred million, cash one hundred million.* Because of that note, even seventy-two million was short. At forty-four it would be shorter still. But the man had sent twenty-eight million abroad last August. Say he had done the same thing a second time, and it would pass.

It would pass.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Kamiya did not press the key a fourth time.

It was not conscience that stopped him.

It was fear. He was afraid of having reached the conclusion *it would pass*. Everything he had learned in thirty years had just moved, all of it, toward taking money out. Where to press so that nothing showed: those thirty years had taught him that.

A man is not honest because he is capable.

Kamiya turned the calculator face down.

Then he went back, in order, over what he had just been thinking.

*Borrow it for now. Put it back in three months. No one is hurt. On paper no one loses anything. Put it back and it never happened.*

He knew those five phrases.

For thirty years he had heard them hundreds of times across the table in branch meeting rooms. The accounts manager who had dipped into the company's money said them. The branch office head who had had his hand in the till said them. Every one of them said them in the same order. *For now, I'll put it back, no one's hurt, on paper, it never happened.*

Not one of them had put it back.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Downstairs, Kayoko turned off the television.

Kamiya put the passbook in an envelope, and put the registered seal in a different drawer. Keeping them apart was a banker's habit. The two in the same place is the most dangerous thing there is.

He kept them apart.

Kept apart, getting at them meant moving his hand twice. Before the second time, he might be able to stop.

The will does not say *divide it in three*. It says: *To anyone who staked it all on one thing, leave not a single yen.*

Kamiya turned off the light.

Last August, someone had moved twenty-eight million out of Soichiro Tono's account. He did not yet know that person's face. But what that person had been thinking, he now knew exactly.

Word for word.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

The next morning, Kamiya telephoned Tachibana's office.

"I have a request about the estate account."

"Yes."

"Could withdrawals be made to require your registered seal as well? I'd like an arrangement that any withdrawal over one million can't be made without two seals — the executor's and yours, as agent."

On the other end, he heard Tachibana set down some papers.

"If an agreement with the bank will do, we can exchange documents. For the two weeks until the trust is set up."

"Yes."

"May I ask why?"

Kamiya was quiet for a moment.

"It isn't good for the executor to be able to move it alone."

"I have never had a request like that before."

"I imagine not."

Tachibana said, "I'll prepare it." She asked nothing more.

They exchanged the papers that afternoon and delivered them to the bank. Afterwards, Kamiya thought about what he had done.

If he trusted himself, he would not need this. The man who can say he doesn't need it is the most dangerous.

Soichiro Tono had not given his son the cash in a lump sum. Eight million a year for ten years. That was not because he did not trust his son. It was to keep trust out of it. Make it so you don't have to rely on trust, and when trust gives out, nothing happens.
