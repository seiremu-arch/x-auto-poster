# Chapter Thirteen: February 5

The Tono Construction office was a prefab building on the old road, south of Ugano.

On the morning of February 26, Kamiya parked outside it. Next door was a materials yard, with scaffolding poles stacked high. It was a spot the rain reached, and still they were stacked square.

Inside, a kettle was singing on the stove. There was one woman in the office, about sixty by the look of her. The boss was out on site, she said.

"My name is Kamiya. I'm the executor. I'd like to see the estimate for demolishing the old house, if I may."

"Oh — the Tonos'."

She brought out a file. She knew exactly where it was.

　　ESTIMATE
　　Re: Tono, Ugano City, ——　Demolition of timber-frame dwelling
　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　¥1,180,000 (excl. tax)
　　Date of issue: February 5, 2026

Kamiya looked at the breakdown.

Temporary scaffolding and sheeting, removal of remaining contents, roof demolition, structure demolition, removal of footings, haulage and disposal of waste, levelling. Eight items. Unit prices and quantities for each. A careful estimate.

Nowhere did the word *handrail* appear.

"Who asked for this estimate?"

"Chizuru-san."

"When?"

"She phoned at the end of January, I think. We went out to look on the 3rd, or the 4th."

The woman turned the pages of the desk calendar.

"The 4th. The boss went on his own."

"Did he go inside?"

"I'd think so. You can't get to that figure without going in to see what's left in there."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Kamiya asked about something else.

"Before a demolition, do you take out the fittings and metalwork?"

"We do. Anything that sells comes out first. Not that there's anything in that house."

"The handrail?"

"Handrail?"

The woman pushed her reading glasses up.

"Nobody pays for a wooden handrail. It'd cost more to cart it away. It just goes under the machine with everything else."

"So you wouldn't take it off before the demolition."

"No."

She was certain.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

On the back wall of the office was a corkboard.

A work schedule, safety slogans, a calendar from the local credit union. Under them, a college notebook hung on a string. On the cover it said *KEYS*.

"What's this?"

"The key log. We hold keys to people's houses, you see. You take one, you write it down; you bring it back, you write it down. The boss is strict about it."

Kamiya said, "May I look?" The woman hesitated, then said, "Don't look at the bits with names on, will you." *Don't look* meant *you may look*.

It was ruled into a table. Date, property, taken by, returned, seal.

He looked at February.

　　2/4　　Tono (old house)　　　Katsumi Tono　　　2/4　　　[seal]

The day the boss went out to look. Returned the same day.

Two lines further down there was another entry.

　　2/8　　Tono (old house)　　　Chizuru Tono　　　2/8　　　[seal]

Kamiya looked for some time at the handwriting in the *taken by* column.

It was the same hand as *Old house — front door* on the envelope in the postbox, and *Altar room — small items* on the cardboard box. Rounded, with short tails.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

"Chizuru-san borrowed the key on February 8."

The woman leaned over to look at the book.

"So she did. Clearing out, I expect. She's borrowed it a few times."

"Which other days?"

The woman turned back a month. Twice in January, once in December. Chizuru's name every time.

"Doesn't Chizuru-san have her own key?"

"She does. But when she comes straight from work she's left her house keys at home, hasn't she. So she stops in here and borrows ours. We've held one for years."

"For years — since when?"

"1998. Since the job when we put the handrail on those stairs."

Kamiya looked up.

"You fitted the handrail?"

"The boss did. Those stairs are steep, aren't they. His wife had bad knees. She couldn't get up, so Mr Tono asked us to put one in."

The woman ran a finger along the back of the calendar, as if tracing something.

"He had it made from wood, specially. Turned by hand, not off the shelf. He said a thin grip would suit his wife's hand better."

"And his wife, afterwards—"

"She died in 2019."

Kamiya thought of the four holes. A little above the waist, evenly spaced, in a straight line.

Someone had taken it off.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

"Do you have a security camera here?"

The woman pointed above the door. A black half-sphere was mounted there.

"We do, but it records over itself every fourteen days. The boss said a cheap one would do."

February 8 was exactly eighteen days ago.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Just after noon, Katsumi came back to the office.

The knees of his work trousers were white with dust. He saw Kamiya, said, "Ah, the executor," and put his helmet down on the desk. A sunburnt face; when he smiled, the lines at the corners of his eyes went deep.

"Did you get a look at the estimate?"

"I did. It's very thorough."

"Even for a job that's only knocking something down, we give honest figures. My brother was that kind of man."

Kamiya stopped slightly at the way he said it. *My brother*, Katsumi had said.

"One question. On February 8, did anyone from here go to the site?"

Katsumi reached for his teacup and stopped.

"The 8th."

"A Sunday."

"We don't work Sundays."

"Nothing taken off in advance, getting ready for the demolition?"

"No. There's no contract yet. We gave an estimate, that's all. We haven't taken the job."

Katsumi lifted the cup and drank.

"Nobody from here went to that site that day."
