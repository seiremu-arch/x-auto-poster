# Chapter Fourteen: One Third

He got the registry certificate for the old factory site on the morning of February 27.

He collected it at the issuing counter of the Legal Affairs Bureau and opened it in the car.

In the owner column there were two names.

　　Soichiro Tono　　share: two-thirds
　　Katsumi Tono　　 share: one-third

Cause: inheritance, 1985. When their father died, the brothers had divided it. The factory building had been in Soichiro's name alone; only the land beneath it had stayed jointly owned, for forty-one years.

Kamiya laid the certificate on his knee and thought for a while.

Jointly owned land cannot be sold by one owner. You can sell your share alone, but hardly anyone is fool enough to buy one-third of a plot. Put the other way: for forty years Katsumi had held a third and never once been able to turn it into money.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

It was past ten when he reached the Tono Construction office.

Katsumi was there today. In the same work clothes as yesterday, he sat on the sofa in the corner of the office and offered Kamiya a chair.

"I've seen the register." Kamiya brought out the certificate. "The factory site is jointly owned with you."

"That's right."

"I'll put only Soichiro's share in the schedule. Two-thirds."

"Understood."

Katsumi glanced at the certificate and handed it straight back. It was the way a man hands back a document whose contents he already knows.

"Forgive me — has there been any talk of selling this share?"

"There has."

He said it plainly.

"Last autumn. A company came up from Tokyo wanting to buy the lot. Said they'd take just my third if that was all they could get."

"At what price?"

"Twenty-eight thousand a *tsubo*. For my part alone, it comes to about twenty-seven million."

Kamiya wrote it down. Close to thirty thousand a *tsubo*.

"You didn't sell?"

"My brother wouldn't."

Katsumi folded his hands on his knees. Thick knuckles; a site man's hands.

"It's joint, you see. If I sell only mine, the buyer's stuck. A third on its own, you can't do anything with it. They said themselves it was no use unless it was three-thirds."

"Why wouldn't Soichiro sell?"

"He didn't give reasons. That's the kind of man he was. He told you what he'd decided, and that was it."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Katsumi got up to make tea.

Kamiya's eyes went, without intending to, to the desk.

Bundles of delivery notes, a calculator, reading glasses, a catalogue of seismic isolation brackets. At the edge lay a business card, face down. The back was uppermost.

Something was scrawled on it in black marker.

　　3/31

With a single line drawn under it.

Katsumi came back with two teacups. Kamiya looked away, but Katsumi seemed to have noticed. He picked up the card, turned it face up, and set it in front of Kamiya.

　　Shirasagi Development Co., Ltd.　　Land Development Division　　Kijima

"That's the Tokyo company."

"And March 31?"

"That's their deadline. They said they'd wind the whole thing up at the end of March."

"Even though the data centre's been cancelled?"

"He rang last week. Said if it could all be put together by the end of March, there was still something to be done."

Katsumi put down his cup.

"I've already turned him down."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

"May I ask about the stakes?"

Katsumi's hand stopped at the rim of the cup.

"There are stakes along the boundary of the site. Red heads. Dated January 20."

"Not ours."

"Then—"

"Shirasagi, I'd say. They'll have sent a surveyor in."

"Can they survey without the landowner's consent?"

"No."

Katsumi said it, and then said nothing more.

*No* meant someone had consented. If not Soichiro, then the man who held the other third, or somebody acting for one of them.

"Did you consent?"

"I did not."

His voice hardened slightly.

"That's the truth. I wanted to sell that land. I'm not hiding that. But I did not let people onto my brother's land behind his back."

Kamiya believed him. He believed him because the man had said *I wanted to sell* first. A liar does not lead with what counts against him.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

When he had finished his tea, Kamiya asked:

"Did you know Soichiro used to go upstairs in the old house?"

"I knew he went on the anniversary. My sister-in-law's tablet's there."

"On the eleventh of every month."

"Ah, the monthly one. Yes, he did say something like that."

Katsumi set his cup down.

"In the daytime, surely. I thought he went at midday, put his hands together a minute, and came home."

"In the evening."

"The evening?"

Katsumi looked surprised.

"He went up those stairs after dark?"

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

As he was leaving, Kamiya asked once more.

"Let me just confirm what you told me yesterday. On February 8, nobody from here went to the old house. Is that right?"

Katsumi had half risen, and stopped.

For a while there was no answer.

"…They shouldn't have."

"Shouldn't have."

"It was a Sunday. Only—"

Katsumi tugged at the cuff of his work jacket, for no reason.

"The young lads, they'll sometimes go and do something off their own bat. Trying to be helpful."

"Such as?"

"Anything. Put sheeting up in advance. Take off whatever comes off."

Yesterday this man had said flatly: *Nobody from here went to that site that day.*

In a day, the way he said it had changed.

"Katsumi-san."

"Yes."

"Did something happen last night?"

Katsumi did not answer.

"Did someone ask you something?"

"No."

"Did you ask someone something?"

Katsumi looked at him. In the sunburnt face, only the skin around the eyes was pale.

"Kamiya-san. You're the executor."

"Yes."

"Your job is to divide up the property."

"Yes."

"Then do that, and only that."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Back in the car, Kamiya wrote in his notebook.

　　2/26　Katsumi: "Nobody went." (flat)
　　2/27　Katsumi: "Shouldn't have." "The lads sometimes help off their own bat."

When he had written it, he looked at the two lines.

When a man changes how he says something overnight, it is usually because he learned something in between. He learned it last night. After Kamiya had seen the key log.

And this man was not saying what he had learned.

*He's covering for someone*, Kamiya thought. He did not write down who. If he wrote it, the fact that he had thought it would remain. For thirty years he had lived by that kind of caution. More than once or twice, that caution had delayed something.
