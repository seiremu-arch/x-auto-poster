# Chapter Three: The Eleventh

The road into Ugano was the same as it had been thirty years ago.

Off the national route onto the prefectural road, over the river, north through the fields. The width of the road and the angle of the bends were exactly as he remembered. What had changed was that the signboards that used to stand out in the paddies were all gone, and that the agricultural co-op building was new.

Coming into the area in front of the station, Kamiya slowed down.

On the corner two streets in there was a three-storey building. The ground floor was an ATM lobby and the bank's name was on the glass. Joshu Akagi Bank, Ugano Branch.

He drove past.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Soichiro Tono's house was toward the south of the town centre.

A new single-storey house. It looked as though it had been built about twenty years ago. The black and white mourning curtain had already been taken down from the gate. Four cars were parked outside, one of them a light truck with *Tono Construction* on the side.

When he slid open the front door, the smell of incense came out.

Several people in black were going back and forth along the hallway. Kamiya gave his name and said, "I was at the Ugano branch thirty years ago."

"You're from the bank?"

The woman who came out was about forty. Still in mourning dress, with an apron over it.

"I'm Chizuru. Tono's eldest daughter."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

He was shown into the tatami room.

The altar was already half cleared: the plain wood memorial tablet, the photograph, and the chrysanthemums were left. The Soichiro Tono in the photograph had a gentler face than Kamiya had expected. The face he had seen once, thirty years ago, had had more edges to it.

He lit incense.

When he turned around there were three people in the room.

One was a man in his mid-forties. His mourning tie was slightly crooked. He sat cross-legged on a cushion and put his phone face down.

One was a woman of about thirty. The mourning dress fitted her. She sat without letting her knees go slack, counting the visitors' teacups.

The last was a man past seventy, not in mourning dress but in a navy work jacket. He was watching the incense smoke.

Chizuru introduced them in turn.

"My brother, Ryosuke."

Ryosuke said, "Hello," from where he sat, then stood up, then sat down again. He seemed unable to decide which it should have been. He shifted his knees twice.

"Sagara-san, from the company."

"Misaki Sagara."

Kamiya's hand stopped short of his teacup.

Sagara.

It was a name he had only ever seen in documents. In the box for the preparer of a trial balance there had been a small seal with one corner chipped off. It was the name on that seal.

*A common enough surname*, he thought. There would be any number of them in Gunma, and in Saitama.

He thought it, and asked nothing.

Having given her name, Misaki took one cup away and carried it toward the kitchen. Nobody had asked her to.

"My father's younger brother, Katsumi."

Katsumi stood up and bowed. A sunburnt face with deep lines at the corners of the eyes. His hands were folded in front of him. The knuckles were thick.

"Thank you for coming all this way."

He was the only one of them looking straight at Kamiya. The other three met his eyes and went back to their hands at once.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Out in the hallway a woman who seemed to be a neighbour said, "Are there another three cushions anywhere?"

"In the cupboard. Bottom right."

Chizuru answered without turning around.

Ryosuke asked the room in general, "Which way's the toilet again?"

"Out of the hall, far end on the left."

Chizuru answered, again without turning.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Chizuru poured tea.

"How did you know my father?"

Kamiya chose his words.

"We dealt with each other once, thirty years ago. On business."

"You were with the bank."

"Yes. I've been retired some time."

Chizuru said, "I see," and did not ask anything further. *A person who doesn't ask,* Kamiya thought. Not out of indifference. She knew what asking would cost the person she asked.

"Where did it happen? Your father."

That was the question he had come to ask.

"At the old house," Chizuru said. "There's an old place up north of here, out in the hamlet. My mother's tablet was in the family altar room there."

"Was he living at the old house?"

"No. He lived here. The old house is empty."

Chizuru went on setting out the cups.

"My father always went to the old house on the eleventh."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Kamiya's hand stopped on the way to his cup.

"The eleventh — meaning?"

"My mother's monthly memorial day. She died on June 11, 2019. Every month after that, on the eleventh."

"Every month?"

"Every month. Rain or snow, he went. So that day as well."

Chizuru's hands did not stop. She set five cups out on the tray and squared up their positions.

"I understand he was found on the 13th."

"Yes. I found him."

"From the 11th to the 13th—"

"I called on the 12th. He didn't pick up. That happened sometimes. His hearing had gone."

Chizuru picked up the tray and stood.

"I went the next morning."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Misaki came back from washing the cup she had taken away. She knew where the kitchen was as well.

Ryosuke's eyes followed her back.

"Sagara-san."

"Yes."

"You're from the company, right."

"Yes."

"Should you be sitting in the family seats?"

The sound in the room cut out for a moment.

Misaki said, "I'm sorry," got off the cushion and sat down again on the bare tatami. That was all.

Chizuru started to say something and stopped. Katsumi said, low, "Ryosuke."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Ryosuke turned his phone over and said:

"Kamiya-san, was it?"

"Yes."

"Did the old man talk about money? Thirty years ago."

"It was a lending matter."

"Huh. How much?"

Misaki set down a cup with a little more sound than necessary. Ryosuke shut his mouth.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Just after four there were voices at the entrance.

A woman with a black document case came in. She looked about fifty-five, and she was not in mourning dress but in a dark navy suit. Chizuru went out to meet her, exchanged two or three words, and then looked back into the room.

"Tachibana."

The woman gave her name and sat down in front of Kamiya.

"I telephoned you."

"You did."

"I had begun to think you would not come."

Mitsuko Tachibana spoke without taking anything out of her case.

"I am holding Mr Soichiro Tono's notarial will. As the family are all here, I will now go through its contents."

The air in the room changed slightly. Ryosuke straightened where he sat.

"Before that, one point for Mr Kamiya."

Tachibana turned to him.

"You have been named as executor of the will."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Nobody said anything.

Ryosuke said, "What?" Chizuru stood holding the tray, not moving. Misaki was looking at her own knees. Only Katsumi was looking straight at Kamiya's face.

"I—"

Kamiya said it. His voice came out lower than he expected.

"I have not seen Mr Tono in thirty years."

"I am aware of that."

Tachibana opened the mouth of her case.

"Mr Tono said the same thing."
