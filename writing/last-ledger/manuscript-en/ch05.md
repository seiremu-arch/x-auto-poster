# Chapter Five: 1996

It was past seven in the evening when he left the Tono house.

Tachibana said there was no hurry over whether he would take on the executorship. She also said that inheritance has deadlines. Renunciation, three months. Inheritance tax, ten months. Nothing could begin until an executor was in place.

"Give me a week."

"That will be fine."

Tachibana handed him a card and went.

Kamiya sat in the car for a while without starting the engine.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

On the way back he drove through the station district.

He stopped the car on the corner, two streets in. Joshu Akagi Bank, Ugano Branch. It was not quite nine at night and only the ATM lobby was lit.

The building had been replaced. Thirty years ago it had been two storeys, with tall narrow windows. There had been a mulberry field behind it, and in winter the dust off the field left the glass clouded white.

His desk had been third from the right along the counter.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

In the spring of 1996, Kamiya was thirty-seven.

His third year in the lending department. The Ugano branch carried about a hundred and fifty borrowers, and some thirty of them were the small factories clustered south of the station. Subcontractors for car parts, farm machinery, construction plant.

Tono Precision had been the second largest of them.

Five years had gone by since the bubble ended. The companies in this town were still holding on. Holding on, but the way they held on had been going quietly wrong.

The first thing he noticed was the inventory.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Tono Precision's monthly trial balance always came in on time.

For a company that size, that alone was unusual. The figures were careful and the accounts were broken down in detail. Kamiya had thought it was a good company.

In the April balance, inventory was up ten per cent on the previous month.

Sales were flat. Purchases were flat. And still the stock rose. There are any number of explanations. A year-end rush on materials. Forward procurement ahead of a production run. A customer slow to take delivery.

Kamiya knew it was none of them.

Pile up inventory and your cost of sales falls. When cost falls, profit appears. On paper, and only on paper.

In the May balance, the inventory had risen again.

Kamiya went to look at it.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

The stock was not in the factory warehouse.

The man who walked him round was the senior managing director. A jacket over his work clothes, about fifty. He stood behind Kamiya the whole time he was counting the racks.

When he had finished counting and turned round, the man was looking at the floor.

"It's short."

When Kamiya said it, the man said, "Yes."

He made no excuse. He did not say why it was short. He only said yes.

Kamiya went back to the branch and wrote the credit memo. It took him three days. On the evening of the third day he put it up to the branch manager.

That same month, head office decided to pull the line.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Tono Precision did not die.

Another regional bank came in, tightened the terms, and carried it. He heard later that the president had covered the bad receivables from the construction company out of his own assets.

In June, the senior managing director died.

Kamiya learned it at the branch morning meeting. The deputy manager said, "Mr Sagara, senior managing director at Tono Precision, has passed away," and nothing else. He did not give the cause. By not giving it, he told everyone.

Kamiya spent that day looking at figures.

He had done the right thing. He had found falsified accounts and reported them. A banker who does not report is not a banker. If he had let those numbers through, the hole would have grown and more people would have gone under. Thirty years on, he had not changed his mind about that.

He had not changed his mind, and still he remembered the exact angle of that man's face when he said yes.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

In September the transfer came through.

A branch in Maebashi. Neither promotion nor demotion, an ordinary move. Kamiya handed over his files and left Ugano.

After that he was transferred four more times, spent two years at head office, and retired from a branch in Takasaki.

For thirty years he did not come back to Ugano.

On the expressway or the national route, he chose roads that did not pass through. He never said the name of the place to his wife or his daughter.

Nobody blamed him. Nobody had any reason to. So for thirty years Kamiya had never had to say anything to himself either.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

It was not quite ten when he got back to the house in Takasaki.

Kayoko was already asleep. Kamiya went up to the six-mat room and undid the cloth.

He took out the ledger and the envelope.

He read the letter again.

　　*For thirty years I have said nothing from my side.*
　　*You said nothing either. I think that was the right way.*

*For thirty years I have said nothing from my side.*

He understood what that line meant now.

Soichiro Tono had known. He had known who stopped the credit. He had known the name, and for thirty years he had said nothing.

*I think that was the right way*, he had written.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Kamiya let the letter rest on his knee.

The trial balance had been prepared by the senior managing director. But a managing director does not think of it on his own. That man would not have broken his own careful figures on his own judgement. The man who said yes in the warehouse had made them because he was told to, and to the end he never gave the name of the man who told him.

For thirty years Kamiya had kept himself from thinking about it.

He had been able to not think about it because Soichiro Tono had said nothing.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

*—If this has reached you, I am already dead.*

In the envelope had been a ledger. A notebook with not one amount of money written in it.

This man was not asking him to protect his money. He was calling in a man who had failed to protect his own.

He had named the banker who cut off his company thirty years ago to stand watch over his money after his death.

If it had been spite, it would have been easier.

Kamiya sat for a while without turning off the light.

He had not been looking for a reason to take it on. He already knew that there was not one reason to refuse.
