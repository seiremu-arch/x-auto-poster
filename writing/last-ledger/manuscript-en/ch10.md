# Chapter Ten: The Winning Side

Kamiya caught up with Ryosuke just outside the office.

"Ryosuke-san. Could you spare me a little time?"

"I'm heading back to Tokyo."

"I'll come with you."

Ryosuke stopped and looked at him.

"What for?"

"To confirm any advance on the inheritance. If your father gave you anything during his lifetime, it has to be reflected in the schedule."

"He didn't give me anything."

"If he gave you nothing, I write that he gave you nothing. That's part of the job too."

Ryosuke was silent for a while, and then said, "Suit yourself."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Takasaki to Tokyo is fifty minutes.

Ryosuke sat by the window with his phone held upright, moving his thumb. On the screen, thin green and red bars stood in a row. Candlesticks. Now and then his thumb stopped, and then moved again.

"What are you looking at?"

"Bitcoin."

"Is it up?"

"It's down."

Ryosuke turned the screen face down and looked out of the window. The Kanto plain went by, flat.

"Did the old man say anything? About me."

"I hadn't seen him in thirty years."

"Right. So you said."

Ryosuke laughed. The way he laughed was a little like the face in his father's photograph.

"I got five million in 2018."

Kamiya took out his notebook.

"When I went independent. I said I needed it to set up the company, and he gave it to me. I didn't sign anything. I never paid it back."

"What was it used for?"

"Office deposit, building the website, and the rest was living costs. It was gone in two years."

"Anything else?"

"No. That was the last. He said it at the time. *This is once*, he said. *Only once.* I didn't understand what *once* meant, back then."

Kamiya wrote *five million*, and the date.

"This goes into the calculation of the statutory share."

"Meaning my share goes down?"

"Meaning it goes into the calculation, that's all. In this case your share doesn't fall below the statutory share, so in practice it makes no difference."

"Then why write it down?"

"I write it down."

Ryosuke said, "Very thorough," and stood the screen up again.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

The flat in Itabashi was eleven minutes' walk from the station.

One bedroom and a living room. Cardboard boxes stacked in the entrance. Four with the tape still uncut. A label for a collagen drink printed on the sides.

"That's from something I used to do."

In the room were three monitors on a desk. Two were black. Only one had a chart moving on it. There was no bed; a futon lay on the floor, unfolded. The curtains were drawn.

Ryosuke sat on the floor and pushed what was on the low table to one side.

"You want to know how much I owe, don't you."

"That's not the executor's business."

"Then what did you come for?"

Kamiya did not answer.

Ryosuke got up and brought a clear plastic folder. He took out the contents and laid them on the table. He did it smoothly. *He has shown these to people many times*, Kamiya thought.

　　Card loans, three companies　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　 4,200,000
　　Margin trading losses (unpaid to securities firm)　　　　7,600,000
　　Top-up margin for crypto (consumer finance)　　　　　　　 5,800,000
　　Borrowed from relatives and acquaintances　　　　　　　　 2,400,000

Twenty million in all.

"Interest?"

"All in, about two hundred and forty thousand a month."

"Income?"

"Three hundred and fifty thousand when there's a job on. Seventy thousand in the months there isn't."

Kamiya looked at the figures. He had seen hundreds of sheets in this shape. There is a shape that can be repaid and a shape that cannot, and this was the one that could not. Unless, that is, a seventy-two-million-yen basket came his way. So he was waiting.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

On one of the monitors a chat window was open.

A column of member names ran down the left. At the top it said *TAKA*. The icon was a photograph of a chair standing in a white room.

"What's this?"

"A community. Thirty thousand a month."

"What does it do?"

"Share a view of the market. TAKA runs a model he built with AI and posts the output every morning. You look at that and decide for yourself."

"Members?"

"Two thousand four hundred."

Kamiya did the sum in his head. Thirty thousand a month times two thousand four hundred was seventy-two million a month.

"How much does he make?"

"On the market?"

"No."

For a moment Ryosuke looked as though he did not understand. Then he looked away.

"…Don't look at it like that."

"I'm sorry."

"I've been a sub-leader since last year," Ryosuke went on. "When somebody new joins, I answer their questions. I get part of the fees. Forty or fifty thousand a month."

"Do you recruit?"

"No. I introduce people. Introducing and recruiting aren't the same thing."

Kamiya knew that way of putting it. He had heard it at branch counters and at head office. Cut it up small enough and none of it is wrong.

At the top of the screen was that day's post.

　　**Stand on the winning side, or stand on the losing side and complain. That's the whole choice.**

"Ryosuke-san."

"Yes."

"When your father's estate comes in, what will you do?"

"Pay it all back. And that's the end of it."

"And after that?"

"After that?"

Ryosuke looked genuinely puzzled.

"After that I'm at zero. I get back to zero. That's good enough, isn't it."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

Leaving, putting on his shoes in the entrance, Kamiya asked:

"Where were you on February 9?"

Ryosuke's hand stopped halfway through passing him the shoehorn.

"The 9th?"

"A Monday."

"Tokyo. Why?"

"The woman next door to the old house says there was a car parked there on the evening of the 9th. A man in his forties, she said."

Ryosuke stood holding out the shoehorn, not moving, for a while. Then he laughed.

"Somebody else, then."

"I see."

"I was in Tokyo. All that week."

Kamiya put on his shoes.

"She said voices got a little loud at the door."

"No idea."

Ryosuke opened the door. One of the fluorescent tubes in the corridor was going, flickering.

"Kamiya-san. You're the executor. You're not a detective."

"That's right."

"Then cut that out."

The door closed.

Outside, the wind came through the alley. Tokyo was a little warmer than Takasaki.

Waiting for the lift, Kamiya opened his notebook and wrote *February 9*. Then, underneath, he added: *Tokyo (his account)*.

For thirty years he had written things that way. *The person says so* does not mean *it is so*.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

The train back was nearly empty.

Kamiya sat by the window, looking at his own face reflected in the dark glass.

He tried to think about the difference between Ryosuke and himself.

Ryosuke was in a thirty-thousand-a-month community, looking at predictions an AI produced, being told to stand on the winning side. He himself had sent twenty-eight million to a firm that said an AI traded automatically in markets around the world, twenty-four hours a day.

The only difference was the amount.

There was one more. Ryosuke had something coming in. His father had died and a cash basket would come to him. Eight million a year, for ten years. His repayments were two hundred and forty thousand a month — two million eight hundred and eighty thousand a year. On paper, he would be saved.

He himself had nothing coming.

Somewhere in Saitama the carriage grew brighter. The face in the window vanished and the lights of a car park outside showed through.

A man with something coming to save him, and a man without. The difference came down to the fact that he was, at this moment, the more wretched of the two. But wretchedness warps judgement. For thirty years he had looked at the paperwork of people whose judgement had warped. Before it warped, every one of them had found one thing to count on.

Kamiya pressed a hand to the inside of his jacket. The executor's seal was there.

The estate account was due to be opened at the start of the following week.
