# Chapter Eight: The Shortfall

On February 18, Kamiya spent the morning going round the banks.

Tachibana had put together the papers proving his authority as executor. A certified copy of the notarial will, Soichiro's closed family register, Kamiya's own seal certificate. Every counter was courteous, and every counter took its time.

For thirty years he had sat on the other side. Seen from the customer's chair, the counter was much further away than he had thought.

By three in the afternoon he had the balance certificates.

　　Joshu Akagi Bank, Ugano Branch　　　　　　　21,400,000
　　Gunma Chuo Credit Union, Head Office　　　　　9,800,000
　　Japan Post Bank　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　3,100,000
　　Kita-Kanto Securities, Ugano (listed shares)　　37,700,000

Seventy-two million in all.

Kamiya laid the four sheets side by side on a coffee-shop table and added them twice. Both times it came out the same.

The Ugano branch of Joshu Akagi Bank stood two streets in from the station.

The building had changed. Where there had been two storeys, there was a new three-storey branch. The mulberry field had gone; it was a coin car park now.

He went in and took a ticket. On the lobby wall, portraits of past branch managers hung in a column. The fourth from the top was a face he knew. The deputy manager in those days — the man who had stamped Kamiya's credit memo. The dates under the photograph said 2009 to 2012.

It was forty minutes before his number was called.

At the securities company he was given the breakdown of the shareholdings. The thirty-seven million seven hundred thousand in listed shares was two electric utilities, a railway, a local food company and two of the big trading houses. Every one of them held for more than ten years. Five years had seen only four trades.

It was an account that did nothing but collect dividends. It did not grow dramatically; nor did it shrink. Kamiya had met many people with accounts like this. As a rule, they were careful people.

Which made it harder to understand.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

He telephoned Tachibana's office.

"Ms Tachibana. What figure did you have in mind for the cash basket?"

"One hundred million."

She answered at once.

"On what basis?"

"A note Mr Tono wrote himself last October. He gave it to me when we were drafting. *Land one hundred million, business one hundred million, cash one hundred million.* He wanted the three split so that each came to about a hundred million."

"May I see the note?"

"You should already have it. I put it in with the papers for the schedule."

It was in his bag. A single sheet of B5 writing paper. Soichiro's hand.

　　Land　　　one hundred million
　　Business　one hundred million
　　Cash　　　one hundred million

Dated October 14, 2025.

"In fact it's seventy-two million."

"…A difference of twenty-eight million."

Tachibana's voice dropped a little. It was the first time Kamiya had heard her lower it.

"He may have sold shares. Though it is not an amount he would need to live on."

"I'll look into it."

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

The next day he went back to the Ugano branch of Joshu Akagi Bank.

An executor may ask for disclosure of the deceased's transaction history. Kamiya had once been on the receiving end of such requests. He remembered the bank's internal rules on what to give and what to hold back. So he also knew what to ask for to get it fastest.

"Deposits and withdrawals, January to December 2025."

It came out in thirty minutes. Nine sheets of A4.

He read them on the sofa beside the counter.

January to July, nothing. Pension, utility bills, director's pay from the company.

In August there was a single line of a different order of magnitude.

　　2025-08-06　　　OVERSEAS REMITTANCE　　　　　28,000,000

Twenty-eight million yen.

Kamiya's finger stopped on that line.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

It took another hour to get the copy of the remittance application. The branch manager came out and checked with someone above him. Thirty years ago, Kamiya had done the same thing.

The copy was on thin carbon paper.

The beneficiary was an account at a bank in Hong Kong. The account name was a company name in Roman letters ending in LIMITED. In the box for the purpose of the remittance it said only *Investment*.

The applicant's signature was in Soichiro Tono's hand. It did not shake.

"Did the counter ask him anything?"

The branch manager chose his words.

"It was a large overseas remittance, so we did confirm. He said himself that it was an investment."

"Did you stop him?"

"…We are not in a position to stop a customer."

Kamiya nodded. He had given the same answer more than once himself.

"Did he say anything about who introduced it?"

The manager left a small pause.

"He said it was an introduction through his daughter's family."

Before leaving the branch, Kamiya looked at the statement once more.

After the overseas remittance on August 6, nothing happened on the account in September or October. The pension went in, the utilities went out, and that was all. No shares sold. No sign of having consulted anyone.

From the month after he sent the twenty-eight million, Soichiro Tono's account had gone back to moving exactly as it had before.

It looked as if nothing had happened. It might be closer to say that he had decided nothing had happened.

On October 14 this man had written *Cash one hundred million* on a sheet of paper. Two months after sending twenty-eight million out of the country.

　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　　※

When he came out, the sun was going down.

Kamiya sat in the car without starting the engine for some time. The carbon copy lay on his knee.

Twenty-eight million.

The same amount he had sent to Arclight Capital.

*Coincidence*, he thought. It's a round figure. A man of this generation putting in most of his retirement money ends up somewhere around there. Not twenty million, not thirty — twenty-eight, an awkward number, is what you get after you subtract what you mean to keep back. That's all it is.

That was all it was, and still his hands would not move.

Soichiro Tono had sent twenty-eight million last August.
Shuji Kamiya had sent twenty-eight million last April.

In the same year, two men had sent the same amount. One was seventy-eight and one was sixty-six. One had run a factory for thirty years and one had examined loans for thirty years.

Neither had been able to stop himself.

There was one more thing that stayed with him.

*An introduction through his daughter's family.*

That was what the manager had said. Chizuru's husband was in the business of brokering financial products. Kamiya had not met him. He had only seen the name, in the relatives column of the schedule. Makoto Kajiwara.

One phone call would settle it. He could ask Tachibana. He could get the records of who Soichiro's mobile had been in contact with around last August. An executor could do that.

Kamiya took out his phone, and put it back in his jacket.

An executor's job is to establish the estate and divide it as the will says. It is not to find out where it was lost. Soichiro had sent the twenty-eight million of his own free will. The papers said so.

He thought that, and knew at the same time that it was running away.

If he asked, there would be an answer. If there was an answer, he would have to tell Chizuru. *Your husband—*

Not yet.

How many cases he had put off with those words over thirty years, he could not remember. The things he put off usually came back later, doubled.

Kamiya put the carbon copy back in its envelope and the envelope in his bag. When he shut the bag, the clasp made a louder sound than he expected.
