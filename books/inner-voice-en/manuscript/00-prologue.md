# Prologue: The loudest voice is not always right

## After the light goes off

During the day you don't hear it. It's covered by work sounds, other people, notifications.

The moment things go quiet, it starts talking. Tomorrow's reply. The way you said that thing. Where you'll be living next year. Something that ended years ago and cannot be changed now.

You pick up the phone from the nightstand. Thirty minutes disappear. You turn it face down. It's still talking.

What are you listening to, in that moment?

Most people would answer: my own thoughts. I thought so too, for a long time. There is one person in my head, and that person worries, blames, and can't decide. That's just what a mind is.

Then one night I wrote it down. I couldn't sleep, so I wrote whatever showed up, about twenty lines, exactly as it arrived.

Reading it the next morning surprised me. It did not look like one person had written it.

*"People will laugh at you."* — that was my mother. Her exact sentence pattern, the one she still uses.

*"You're too old to be starting over."* — that was a manager I worked for ten years ago. Apparently I can still reproduce his delivery with some accuracy.

*"What about next month, what about next month."* — that one wasn't anybody. It just repeated itself with slightly different wording.

And down near the bottom of the page, one line was in a completely different register.

*"Maybe that's enough."*

Short. Quiet. It appeared once and did not come back.

## There is more than one person in there

What I learned that night is that the talking in my head is not one voice.

And that as long as I believed it was one, the loudest voice automatically became "what I think."

We choose by volume, most of the time. A loud voice sounds like an important message. A voice that repeats itself sounds like it must have a reason to repeat.

That design was correct to begin with. A warning has to be loud, fast, and repetitive. If a child steps toward the road, a calm and gentle voice will not get there in time.

But almost nothing in a normal week is a car. What there is, instead, is unanswered messages, deadlines, and something a colleague said in passing. That loud voice fires at the same volume for all of it.

As long as you're choosing by volume, the same voice wins every time.

## If you go looking, you won't find it

There's a phrase I've never been comfortable with: *find your inner voice.*

If you go looking, you'll usually find something. The trouble is that what turns up that way tends to match what you were hoping for. You go somewhere quiet, close your eyes, and ask yourself what you really want. Something answers. You decide that something was your inner voice.

But what answered was almost certainly **whichever voice most wanted to talk.**

So this book doesn't use the word *find*. It uses **tell apart**.

The voices are all already there. What's missing isn't another voice. It's a way of separating the ones you've got.

And there is only one thing you can actually do to separate them.

> Your quietest voice never gets louder. So the only way to hear it is to get quieter yourself.

You can treat that sentence as the whole book. Everything after it is just how.

## The four voices

Chapter 1 sorts the talking into four.

- **The anxious voice** — fast. Repeats. Usually speaks in the future tense
- **The *should* voice** — uses the vocabulary of correctness. "Normally." "Properly." "At your age."
- **The borrowed voice** — somebody else's phrasing, intact. It has an accent
- **The quietest voice** — slow. Short. Says it once

Sorting them into four is not about picking the right one. It's about **counting how many are in the room.**

Counting alone makes it quieter. It's the same in a crowd: just knowing who is speaking changes how loud a room feels.

## How to use this book

**Get a paper notebook.**

You can't write in an ebook. And the practices here work partly because you read back what you wrote. A half-used notebook is fine. Scrap paper stapled together is fine. A notes app on your phone is not the best choice, and I'll explain why in Chapter 4.

**Read the chapters in order.**

The first three build the criteria for telling voices apart (counting → speed → the body). Chapters 4 and 5 build the conditions for hearing anything at all (room → questions). Chapters 6 and 7 deal with what happens after you hear something (deciding small → coming back). It stacks, so skipping makes the later parts weaker.

**One practice at a time.**

Each chapter ends with one practice, five to fifteen minutes, seven in total. One chapter a week is, if anything, fast. Doing one of them for three weeks will serve you better than doing all seven and stopping on day three.

**Reading is enough.**

You can finish this book without doing a single practice. If, the next time something starts talking in your head, you think *ah, that's the borrowed voice* — most of what this book is for has already happened.

## Who this book is probably not for

It seems kinder to say this early.

**If you need a clear answer quickly**, this book is too slow. What's in here is a procedure for waiting, counting, and waiting again. If you have to decide something this week, it won't arrive in time.

**If you want evidence**, this will disappoint you. I'm writing from my own records, not from research. What I counted was lines in my own notebook, and that's all it is.

**If you want spiritual language to rest in**, this may not be your book either. There's no universe in here, no vibrations. The reason is simple: I can't verify them. And if I state one unverifiable thing as fact, that line costs me the parts you *could* have checked.

On the other hand, if you've ever had a thought that was supposedly yours and somehow sounded like someone else — that's the door in. That feeling is all I started with too.

## What this book does not promise

**It does not promise results.** Quiet behaves more like a skill, and how fast it arrives varies a lot from person to person. Some people change quickly. Some have six months where nothing happens. I was the second kind.

**It is not a substitute for medical or psychological care.** What's here is a way for a healthy person to sort out their own thinking. It is not treatment. If you haven't been sleeping for days, if you can't stop crying, if your body won't move in the morning — close this book and talk to a professional. That isn't a detour. It's the shortest route.

**It will not try to make you a calm person.** Quiet is not a personality. Loud days will keep coming. What this book is trying to hand you is a **place to come back to** on the loud days.

## At the end of this chapter

The loudest voice is not always right.

We'll start by counting how many there are.
