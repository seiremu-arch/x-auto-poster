# Back matter

## The seven practices — steps only

Paper and a pen is all the equipment. You don't need to do all of them.

**Practice 1 — Write the voices down (10 min, Ch. 1)**
Write one line about what's on your mind. Write ten lines of whatever shows up, unedited. Mark the left edge: **A** (anxious) / **S** (*should*) / **B** (borrowed) / **?** (don't know). Copy only the **?** lines onto a separate page.

**Practice 2 — The twenty-four hour rule (10 sec + 3 min, Ch. 2)**
Don't answer on the spot. Say you'll come back, with a date attached ("let me check my week and get back to you tomorrow"). Write the answer you have now, in one line. Twenty-four hours later, **before reading that line**, write today's answer, then compare.

**Practice 3 — Try both options in your body (5 min, Ch. 3)**
First spend thirty seconds noting where your chest, throat, shoulders and breath usually sit. Turn each option into one short sentence and **say it out loud.** Wait ten seconds and check the four places. Walk to reset, then do the other one. Look only at which loosened.

**Practice 4 — Seven minutes of doing nothing (7 min, Ch. 4)**
Phone out of reach. Seven minutes with no new input. Walking, thinking and checking the time are all allowed. Afterwards write one line: **loud** or **quiet.** The first three minutes being loud is part of the procedure.

**Practice 5 — The three-part question (15 min, Ch. 5)**
Divide the page in three. (1) What happened — past tense, facts only; cross out the adjectives and carry them into part two. (2) What's still catching — present tense. (3) What could I do tomorrow — small. Replace every *why* with *what / where / since when*.

**Practice 6 — The smallest possible step (5 min, Ch. 6)**
Write the thing you can't decide, in one line. Write the smallest action that would count as movement. If it won't finish in seventy-two hours, cut it again. Write when you'll do it, as a date and time. If no starting step comes, look for a **stopping step.**

**Practice 7 — Three lines to come back (30 sec, Ch. 7)**
One thing you can see / one thing you can hear / one thing your body is touching. Don't close your eyes. Nothing to write. Tie it to one place so it needs no decision.

## The four voices

| Voice | Speed | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The anxious voice | within three seconds | repeats; speaks in the future tense |
| The *should* voice | seconds to minutes | "normally," "properly"; conclusions that benefit nobody |
| The borrowed voice | minutes to hours | somebody else's phrasing, intact; it has an accent |
| The quietest voice | overnight to days | short; says it once; present tense; arrives as observation |

## One request, and one disclaimer

**This book is not a substitute for medical or psychological care.**

What's in here is a set of procedures for a healthy person to sort out their own thinking. It is not treatment.

If you haven't slept for days, if your body won't move in the morning, if you can't stop crying, if your mood hasn't come back for weeks, if a physical symptom persists — close this book and talk to a doctor or another professional. If you feel worse after doing a practice, that practice isn't for you, so stop doing it.

None of the ways of telling things apart in this book are for interpreting physical symptoms. When in doubt, err toward being seen.

**Nothing here is promised.** Quiet behaves like a skill, and how quickly it arrives varies a great deal between people. Having six months where nothing changes is not unusual.

## Afterword

This book started with twenty lines written on a night I couldn't sleep.

That night, I believed I was thinking something over by myself. Reading it back the next morning and finding that at least four people had been talking was, I remember, a considerable relief. I hadn't been struggling with a decision. I'd been sitting through an argument.

Since then, all I've been doing is putting names to that argument and counting. Nothing more specialized than that. What's in this book is the counting, and how to make enough quiet to be able to count.

How many are talking in your head right now. If even one more night arrives when you can tell, then this book has done what it was for.

Thank you for reading.

Kazu A. Suzuki
