# Closing: Quiet is not a destination

## What changed in a year

It's been a year since I started writing this.

Honestly: I have not become a calm person.

The talking still starts after the light goes off. I still think about unanswered messages, and something I said badly ten years ago still surfaces. The anxious voice arrives in the same quantity it did a year ago. It has not decreased.

One thing changed.

**The time it takes me to recognize *that's the anxious voice* got shorter.**

It used to take until morning. Now it takes about thirty minutes. Some days I can think *ah, there it is* as it starts.

That's all it is, but the distance between thirty minutes and until-morning turned out to be much larger than I expected. Once you've recognized it, it stops being your conclusion and becomes one of four people talking. You can leave it to talk.

## The four voices don't go away

Chapter 1 sorted the talking into four.

Back then, some part of me assumed a day would come when the anxious voice and the *should* voice were gone, and only the quietest one remained.

It didn't come. I don't think it's coming.

The anxious voice has a job as a lookout, and some days it's needed. The *should* voice remembers the shape of living alongside other people. And mixed into the borrowed voice are the phrasings of people I don't want to be without. I'm not asking for a head with none of my mother's sentences in it.

**Don't erase them. Just be able to count them.**

If you know there are four in the room, then one of them talking loudly is a quarter of the opinion. It's only while you believe it's one person that it is all of it.

What this book was trying to do was never erasing. It was separating.

## Quiet turned out to be a place to return to, not a place to arrive

Let me write the sentence once more.

> Your quietest voice never gets louder. So the only way to hear it is to get quieter yourself.

What a year taught me is that *getting quieter* there is **an action, not a state.**

You don't arrive at a quiet state and take up residence. Every time it gets loud, you come back. You speed up again, and you come back again. There is nothing else to it.

So quiet was not a destination. It was **the place you come back to.**

Treat it as a destination and failing to arrive looks like failure. Treat it as a place you return to and leaving is part of the design. Leave, come back. Keep doing only that, and you're using this book correctly.

## What this book did not say

Let me list what I left out. Not because I avoided it — because I don't have the answers.

**Why that voice can be called "yours"** is something I can't explain. Slow, short, says it once, the body loosens. I can write the features. Where it comes from, I don't know. Some people call it the soul; some call it nothing but nerves firing. I didn't take either side, because **you can write the procedure for telling voices apart without taking one.**

**Fate and meaning** aren't in here either. I never wrote that following this voice puts you on the right path. It doesn't, necessarily. I've followed my quietest voice into choices that didn't work out. What makes them easier to live with isn't that they were right — it's that I know it was me who chose.

**Talent** I stayed away from too. Write it as though some people can get quiet and others can't, and the people who couldn't have been handed something to blame themselves with. As far as I can tell, this isn't aptitude. It's repetitions.

Some readers will find the book thin because of what's missing. That's all right. **Not stating unverifiable things as fact was the one promise I kept.**

## How many times, in a year

I counted once, so here are the numbers. They may be useful.

- Practice 1 (writing the voices down) — twelve times
- Practice 2 (the twenty-four hour rule) — about forty
- Practice 3 (trying both options in the body) — about twenty
- Practice 4 (seven minutes) — twice a week when running; around sixty, excluding the gaps
- Practice 5 (the three-part question) — twenty-five
- Practice 6 (the smallest possible step) — thirty
- Practice 7 (three lines to come back) — never counted. Over a thousand, probably

As you can see, it's weighted toward the back. And there are three gaps of about two months in there.

**This much is enough** is my honest impression. Daily was never necessary.

## If you take one thing

Seven practices, and I've said more than once that you don't need all of them. I mean it.

If you take one, take **the three lines from Practice 7.**

One thing you can see. One thing you can hear. One thing your body is touching.

Thirty seconds, no verdict, works while exhausted. In the stretches when the other six are impossible, this is what stays. And on a day when you've come back through these three lines, you can remember the others. That order is the more natural one, I think.

## Last

If something starts talking tonight after the light goes off, try one thing.

**How many are talking right now?**

Just count. You don't have to produce an answer. You don't have to decide which one is real.

While you're counting, the room gets a little quieter. And in that quiet, a seat opens up for the smallest voice.

Whether it comes is up to the day.

More nights than not, it won't. Even so, the nights you left the seat open do stay with you.
