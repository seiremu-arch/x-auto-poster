# Style rules — Your Quietest Voice

日本語版 `books/inner-voice/STYLE.md` と同じ立場を、英語で守るためのもの。
**読者が自分で確かめられることだけを書く。**

## Voice

- First person "I". The reader is "you"
- Never tell the reader what state they are in. Not "you are exhausted" but "when you are exhausted"
- Don't instruct from above. Prefer "try" / "here is what I do" over "you must"
- When describing experience, include the times it did not work. No success-only stories

## Words we do not use

Unverifiable language. Not in the book, not on the cover, not in the description.

> the universe / vibrations / manifest(ing) / the law of attraction / energy (even as metaphor) /
> awakening / higher self / your true self / soul's purpose / divine / the subconscious
> (without defining it) / ego (without defining it)

Also avoid absolute claims: **always / never (about results) / everyone / guaranteed /
will change your life / proven**.

One exception, same as the Japanese edition: the prologue's line declaring that this book
does not talk about the universe or vibrations. Saying the word once to disown it is
not the same as leaning on it.

```bash
grep -niE "the universe|vibration|manifest|law of attraction|higher self|your true self|soul's purpose|awakening|guaranteed|will change your life|proven" \
  books/inner-voice-en/manuscript/*.md
```

## Terms (keep these stable across chapters)

| Concept | Term | 日本語版 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the four voices | the anxious voice / the *should* voice / the borrowed voice / the quietest voice | 不安の声 / べき論の声 / 借りものの声 / 内なる声 |
| criterion 1 | speed (the order things arrive in) | 速さ |
| criterion 2 | the body — loosens or tightens | 体 — ゆるむ / 締まる |
| what we make | room (not silence) | 余白 |
| what we do | counting, telling apart, coming back | 数える / 聞き分ける / 戻る |

Never call the quietest voice "intuition", "your gut", or "your higher self". Those words
carry claims this book does not make.

## Sentences

- One idea per sentence. If a sentence holds two, split it
- 3–4 sentences per paragraph. Put the point in the first one
- Open scenes with a concrete moment, not an abstract noun. Not "anxiety is" but "at 11pm, after the light is off"
- Two metaphors per chapter, maximum
- Only **procedures** may be stated flatly. "Get a sheet of paper" is fine as a command

## Safety

- Never let a reader interpret a physical symptom through these practices
- Never write anything that reads as "you don't need to see a doctor"
- Say plainly that if sleep, appetite, or mood do not come back, the practices stop and a
  professional starts. Put this in Chapter 3 and in the back matter
